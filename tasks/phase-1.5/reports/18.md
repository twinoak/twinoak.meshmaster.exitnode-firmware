# p1.5-18 — One wake

The loop has a second way to be driven. `AgentLoop::loop()` is still the Heltec,
called for ever from the Arduino loop with WiFi up, and **not one byte of what it
does changed**. `AgentLoop::wake(intent, budget)` is a device that sleeps: one
pass of the same steps in the same order — config if this uplink has none, the
heartbeat with the agent's own promise, the offline buffer, the claim sized from
the cap, the jobs each priced before they go on the air, the batches — each
bounded in seconds and in energy, and then it returns and the caller sleeps for
the interval the server accepted. Nothing of it spins and nothing of it restarts
the board: a backoff is a wait the report hands back, and the reboot guard is
**told** rather than taken.

The whole thing runs on a workstation against pile 1.5's fakes, and every bound
is asserted on the clock the test moves: **356 native cases in 22 suites, all
passing** — `master`'s 337 with no assertion changed, plus 17 wake cases in
`test_loop` and 2 heartbeat cases in `test_envelopes`. Both boards build with
`-Werror`; RAM is **48 bytes** larger and flash **1 264** (V3) and **1 280**
(V4), every byte of it named below, and the linker drops `wake()` itself from the
board image because nothing there calls it.

`spec/EXIT-NODES.md` §5, §6 and §10 were read in full from
`twinoak/meshmaster.dk` at today's `origin/master`, **`0595dc2`** — which is pile
4.14 complete, task 121 (*jobs that wait for a wake*) included, one commit past
what task 17's report found. Proposal 01 parts B and C and `spec/SYSTEM.md` §5
were read from `twinoak.meshcore.standard-repeater-design-v2`, and
`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §10 and `tasks/phase-3/05-the-wake-cycle.md` from
`twinoak.meshcore.walter-firmware` — the seam this loop will sit behind
(`Backend::exchange(what_we_know, deadline)`) and the wake it will be step 8 of.

## The six steps, and what each is bounded by

| Step | When it runs | What stops it |
|---|---|---|
| **config** | this uplink has no config yet, or `onLinkUp()`/`reconfigure()` made one due. After a deep sleep the object is gone with the RAM, so this is **once a boot and not once a wake** — which is what "the config if stale" means for something that sleeps | the uplink's seconds. A failure is noted and the wake goes on: the heartbeat answers the budget, the interval and the clock every time, and it is the call the wake exists for |
| **heartbeat** | always, unless a 401 has halted the loop | the uplink's seconds. It carries the intent; its reply's `pollIntervalS` is **the accepted interval** and is in the report, and `takeServerTime` happens here — before the first job needs a timestamp |
| **results** | the offline buffer is not empty | the uplink's seconds, and the **byte cap**: a body that will not fit stays with the timestamps and the client id it was made with. A server that refuses is a failure and backs off; a byte cap is not |
| **claim** | the heartbeat said `jobsWaiting` | the **transaction cap**: `max` is never more than `kClaimMax` and never more than the cap can hold at `kTransactionsPerJobAtLeast` (2 — a login and one request, the cheapest shape §7 has above a trace). A cap with no room for one job makes **no claim at all** |
| **jobs** | the claim handed some over | each job is priced by `mm::transactionsOf` (§7's table through `transactionsFor`, plus the login and the ACL restore) and by `estimateJobMs` (those transactions at the budget's pace, one reply wait each). One that will not fit the seconds or the cap is `failed` with *wake budget*, at no transaction and with nothing sent |
| **observations** | the ring is not empty | the uplink's seconds and the **byte cap**: as many batches as it allows, oldest first, each posted whole. What does not fit stays in the ring with the moment each advert was heard |

And the three things a wake subtracts from a pass: a 401, a backoff or a
`Retry-After` **returns** instead of spinning (`report.sleepAtLeastS` is what is
left of it, and the next wake honours it); a heartbeat that did not come back
stops the wake there, because there is no accepted interval, no `jobsWaiting` to
believe and nothing to gain by putting the rest on a link that just failed; and
the reboot guard says *the platform would have been asked to reboot*
(`report.rebootWanted`, with the reason) instead of asking.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The transaction cap is the intent's and is not a second field of `Budget`.**
  The task lists `Budget` as "seconds for the whole wake, seconds for the uplink,
  a transaction cap (the intent's), and a byte cap" — and the parenthesis is the
  reason not to put it there. `maxTransactionsThisWake` is *stated to the server*
  in the heartbeat and *enforced here* against the same arithmetic; two fields
  holding one number can disagree, and then the board would promise the server
  one cap and keep another. `Budget` therefore has `wakeSeconds`,
  `uplinkSeconds` and `postBytes`, and says in as many words that the cap is
  `WakeIntent::maxTransactionsThisWake`. A reviewer who wants the field there
  should say so; it is three lines and a `static_assert` away.
- **The task's fixture instruction could not be followed literally.** "Add a
  second fixture there" means a fixture in `twinoak/meshmaster.dk`, written by
  `MeshMaster.Contracts`' round-trip tests; a session of this repository may not
  write one (the fixtures are copied, never edited here, and `tasks/README.md`
  says what to do when a task needs something the other repository owns). The
  expectation was anchored to the fixture instead, by cutting its last two
  members off, and the request is in `FOLLOWUPS.md` for Thomas.
- **A wake makes a config call *and* a heartbeat even when the config call
  failed**, which means two failures against a server that is away rather than
  one. `loop()` would have stopped at the hold the first failure set. A device
  that wakes four times a day has one chance per wake, and the heartbeat is the
  call that carries the promise, the clock and `jobsWaiting`; stopping the wake
  because a startup nicety did not answer would waste the whole slice. The two
  failures are counted honestly and the backoff is what the caller sleeps
  through.
- **`doObservations` and `flushResults` changed shape**, which the task did not
  ask for: a byte cap cannot be applied from outside a function that builds the
  body it bounds. `flushResults()` keeps its signature and delegates;
  `doObservations()` became `doObservations(size_t&, bool*)` and `loop()` passes
  `kNoByteCap` and no flag, which is exactly what it did before. The `bool*` is
  so that a wake can tell "the cap would not take this batch" from "the server
  refused it" and report the right word.
- **`AgentLoop::Counters` moved to namespace scope as `AgentCounters`**, with
  `using Counters = AgentCounters;` inside the class so that every existing
  caller is untouched: a `WakeReport` and a `WakeState` both carry a copy and
  neither is a member of the loop. `MeshCounters` is the same shape in `IMesh.h`.
- **`claimAndRun` was split rather than copied.** A wake has to decide between
  the claim and each job, so `JobRunner` grew `claim(max)`, `claimed(i)`,
  `runClaimed(i)`, `reportNotRun(i, why)` and `endClaim()`, and `claimAndRun()`
  is those five in order. The body of the per-job loop moved into `runClaimed`
  **character for character**, which is why `test_buffers`' reading of this
  file's text (`Outcome outcome = runJob(job, ctx, dataLen);` before
  `posted = post(job.id, g_body, len);`) still passes untouched.
- **For the other repository**: the fixture above, and nothing else. Nothing of
  `EXIT-NODES.md` §5, §6 or §10 disagreed with what was built. Two things in
  §6's *as built* notes are the server's and are deliberately **not** mirrored
  here: `AgentLimits.MinCheckInS` / `MaxCheckInS` (the loop proposes what it is
  given and reads the accepted value out of the reply, rather than clamping
  twice — "a heartbeat is not a form"), and `JobDefaults.TransactionsOf`'s
  per-job pricing at the claim, which this loop mirrors only well enough to size
  `max` and to refuse a job it cannot run.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **"The config if stale" is "if this uplink has no config".** The task does not
  say what stale means. A device that deep-sleeps loses the `IUplink` object with
  its RAM, so `hasConfig()` is false on every boot and true for every later wake
  of that boot: one call a boot, no timer, and `onLinkUp()`/`reconfigure()` still
  make it due as they always did. A device that stays up and calls `wake()` in a
  loop gets one config call and no more, which is `loop()`'s rule too.
- **`kTransactionsPerJobAtLeast = 2`.** The claim is sized before any type is
  known, so the cap has to be divided by *something*: the cheapest job that needs
  a session is a login and one request, and the only thing below it in §7 is a
  trace at class `none`. Sizing at 2 under-asks for a wake of traces, which is
  the right way round for a battery, and the server caps precisely anyway because
  it knows the types (task 121).
- **A job is priced generously.** `transactionsOf` adds the ACL restore wherever
  the claim carries a grant, not only at class `admin`, and `estimateJobMs` is
  the transactions times the gap plus one reply wait each. An estimate a battery
  spends against may be too high and must never be too low. `neighbours` of
  unknown length is priced at one page, as `transactionsFor` prices it — the
  server prices three from the last snapshot and neither of us can know from the
  claim.
- **A result that a wake could not run is posted outside the byte cap.** Its body
  is a couple of hundred bytes and answering the lease comes first: "never left
  leased and silent" is the rule the cap must not be able to break.
- **A batch too long for the byte cap is shortened, not refused.** The length is
  only known once the JSON is written, so a *fresh* batch's count comes down by
  half until the body fits; a cap smaller than one item posts nothing and the ring
  keeps everything. A batch that was already built and refused is **not**
  shortened — it must go out again under the client id it was built with, or the
  server would de-duplicate on that id and the items the shorter one does not
  carry would be consumed unsent. (The first version of this did shorten it, and
  `AByteCap…` caught it: the next wake inherited a count of one and posted twenty
  adverts in two batches.)
- **`WakeState` holds durations, not deadlines**, and a `version`. `millis()`
  starts again at zero after a deep sleep, so `holdForMs` is what was left of a
  wait and `failingForMs` is how long the present run of failures has lasted;
  `restore()` re-bases both against the clock it finds. `lastHeartbeatAtMillis_`
  is deliberately *not* restored: it is this boot's uptime clock and the console's
  "x s ago" would be a lie.
- **The report's `why` is a static string and the steps are an enum.** Five words
  for a step (`skipped`, `done`, `failed`, `no time`, `no budget`) are the
  Walter's own vocabulary for a wake record (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §10's *no
  time*), and a `const char*` the caller may log costs no buffer. There is no
  `WakeReport::describe()`: the one-line record is the consumer's journal format
  and not this library's.
- **`heartbeatIfOverdue` still works inside a wake.** `wake()` sets
  `heartbeatScheduled_` before the heartbeat, so a job that outlasts the accepted
  interval sends the one that fell due from its own wait, exactly as on the board.
  At an interval of an hour that never happens, which is the point of proposing
  one.
- **The intent is a member of the loop**, read by `buildHeartbeat`, because
  `test_seams` holds that function's signature and the heartbeat must be built in
  one place for both ways of driving the loop. `loop()` never sets it, so it is
  zero there and neither field is written.
- **`FakeUplink` learned two things**, both of them the server's own behaviour:
  a claim hands over at most its `max` and **leaves the rest queued** (task 121:
  what is left behind stays queued and bound to nothing), and the `max` of every
  claim is recorded so a test can read what the cap asked for. `forgetQueued()` is
  for the property test, which runs round after round against one fake server.

## New libraries (with the one-line justification)

None. One new standard header in the library, `<type_traits>`, for the

it to retained storage as bytes assumes.

## Not verified here (and why)

**Nothing the Heltec does changed**, and `wake()` is not in its image, so the
bench list is short and is a *re*-check. What is genuinely unverified is the
mode itself: no device has ever called it.

1. **A real wake on a Walter** — its phase 4, which implements `IMesh` over the
   link, `IUplink` over the modem and `IPlatform` over ESP-IDF and wires this
   into step 8 of `WakeCycle` (`twinoak.meshcore.walter-firmware`,
   `tasks/phase-3/05-the-wake-cycle.md`). Three things only a real unit can
   answer: whether `Budget::uplinkSeconds` is the right bound for an LTE attach
   that took three goes; whether the interval the server accepts and the interval
   the battery wants ever disagree enough to matter; and whether
   `estimateJobMs`'s pace-dominated estimate is good enough when the mesh is a
   link client and every transaction carries a request/response over a UART as
   well.
2. **The two heartbeat fields against the real server.** `nextCheckInS` and
   `maxTransactionsThisWake` are held to the contract fixture here and to
   `AgentLimits`' clamping nowhere: the first unit to send `nextCheckInS: 3600`
   is what proves the exit node's page reads *asleep until about 14:30 UTC* and
   that `agents.last_poll_interval_s` took the accepted value. meshmaster.dk's
   tasks 120 and 121 have their own tests for that side.
3. **One job, any type, on the bench** — the walk of `docs/console.md`, as 17's
   report asked for and no report has yet produced. The two things to watch that
   this task touched: the `jobs` and `server` console reports (a job answered
   without going on the air would appear in `jobs` with 0 transactions, and
   `ran()` deliberately does **not** count it), and that a claim still ends with
   every credential wiped — `endClaim()` is where that happens now, and
   `test_runner`'s `TheCredentialBytesAreZeroAfterTheJob` is what holds it on the
   host.
4. **A wake on a board at all.** `pio run -e heltec_v3` proves it compiles for an
   ESP32-S3; the one-wake path is dead code there. The first firmware to link it
   live is the Walter's, and the first thing to check there is the
   `static_assert` on `WakeState` under the IDF's own standard setting.
