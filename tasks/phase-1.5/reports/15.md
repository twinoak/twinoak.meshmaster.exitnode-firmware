# p1.5-15 — The seams

Three interfaces now stand between the agent's job logic and the board it runs
on, and a fourth lets another firmware compile its own job types in beside the
thirteen. `JobRunner` and `AgentLoop` talk to `IMesh`, `IUplink` and
`IPlatform` (`src/agent/IMesh.h`, `IUplink.h`, `IPlatform.h`) and to nothing of
Arduino, the ESP-IDF, WiFi, NVS or MeshCore; `AgentMesh`, `AgentClient` and a
new `Esp32Platform` (`src/platform/`) are the Heltec's implementations, wired in
`main.cpp`. The settings the loop read for the heartbeat come in as values
(`HeartbeatIdentity`, filled by `main.cpp` and refreshed on `set radio.*`), the
runner's twenty file-static buffers are one `JobRunner::Scratch` the runner
owns, `sendCli` draws the sender timestamp and hands it back instead of taking
one, and the `MeshReply` the runner reads is the library's own struct, sized by
two constants that `AgentMesh.h` holds against MeshCore's at compile time.

**The Heltec does the same thing.** Every one of the 256 existing native test
cases passes with no test file changed — including the five suites that hold
`JobRunner.cpp`'s text — two new suites add fourteen more, both boards build with
`-Werror`, RAM moved by 160 bytes, and the one frame-level change (where the CLI
timestamp is drawn) draws from the same `getCurrentTimeUnique()` series one call
later, so the bytes on the air are the same. Done in one session.

The proposal this pile builds is
`twinoak.meshcore.standard-repeater-design-v2`'s
`software/spec/proposals/03-agent-core-as-a-library.md`, read at `main` =
`365f1ebc155633f8ea5fb4e3a005b8ffb8e818f2` together with its
`software/spec/LINK-PROTOCOL.md` §6 (and §7, see below). meshmaster.dk's
`spec/API.md` §3 (the extend and progress calls) was read from the local clone
at `d45594aa` (one commit ahead of its `origin/master`); the two calls' shapes
are the four fixtures already in `tests/fixtures/contracts/`.

## What is on `AgentMesh` and not on `IMesh`, and why

- **`jobInFlight()`** — nobody calls it (the runner knows whether it began a job). Kept on `AgentMesh` for the console's future; not a thing the runner needs.
- **`setFrameLog(bool)`, `frameLog()`** — `log.frames` is the serial console's switch on the in-process mesh's `logFrame`; the Walter's RAK prints its own frames on its own port (or not). Board.
- **`recent(i)`, `recentCount()`** — the `mesh` command's last five adverts *with their names*, the one place a stranger's name is read, by a person. Console, not job logic.
- **`payloadTypeName(type)`** — the frame log's words for MeshCore's `PAYLOAD_TYPE_*`. Board.
- **`kFloodPathHashSize`** — how *this* mesh floods (two-byte hashes). The RAK makes the same choice for its own floods (§6, "floods the client originates use two-byte path hashes"), so it is a rule of each implementation and not a parameter of the interface.
- **The `mesh::Mesh` surface** — `self_id`, `begin()`, `getRTCClock()`, the radio: MeshCore's, and what `main.cpp` sets up. The library never touches an identity or a radio.
- **The protected overrides** — `onRecvPacket`, `onAdvertRecv`, `onPeerDataRecv`, `onPeerPathRecv`, `onTraceRecv`, `searchPeersByHash`, `getPeerSharedSecret`, `logRx`, `logTx`, `allowPacketForward`: the adapter's inside, where MeshCore calls up. On the Walter this is the RAK's firmware.
- **`kReqTypeGet*`** moved *to* `IMesh.h` as namespace constants (the runner sends them); `kReqTypeGetAccessList` was already beside its parser in `ReplyCodec.h` and stayed there.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The size criterion** ("they differ by less than 1 kB") is met for RAM and
  not for flash, which is 10 kB *smaller* for the reason in the checklist, with
  code and rodata 6.9 kB *larger*, every kilobyte of it named there. The
  proposal's "to within the cost of three vtables" is not what the task's own
  deliverables cost: the two uplink calls the reference agent never makes are
  2 kB on their own, and the proposal did not count them. I proceeded rather
  than stop; the reviewer may disagree.
- **`Stream&` left the portable files.** The task names `Arduino.h`,
  `esp_system.h`, `WiFi`, `Preferences` and MeshCore; `<Stream.h>` is
  Arduino's too and 16 could not have moved `JobRunner.h` with it. The four
  console reports (`jobs`, `sessions`, `server`, `errors`) now write to a
  `TextOut` (`src/agent/TextOut.h`: `write`, with `print`/`println`/`printf`
  that make Print's bytes — `"\r\n"` line ends, `vsnprintf` formatting), and
  `main.cpp` wraps `Serial` in `StreamOut` (`src/console/StreamOut.h`). The
  console's output is byte for byte the same; `Seams.TextOutWritesWhatAStreamWouldHave`
  holds the bytes.
- **`IUplink` carries three things the task's list did not**: `configured()`
  (the loop gates every call on it), `link()` — a `LinkState {up, connects,
  drops}` — because the loop gated on `wifi_->connected()` and printed
  `connects()`/`drops()`, and the task's `AgentLoop::begin` has no `WifiLink`;
  and `limiter()` (the `server` report prints the 120-a-minute window). The
  WiFi client answers them from `WifiLink`, which `AgentClient::begin` now
  takes. `onWifiUp()` is `onLinkUp()`.
- **`IPlatform` carries `selfReboots()`** beside `rebootForHealth(why)`: the
  heartbeat's `reboots` counter is `Health.h`'s RTC count, and a platform that
  reboots for health is the one that counts it. **The clock half is its own
  interface, `IClock`** (`hasTime`, `now`, `fromNetwork`, `sourceName`,
  `takeServerTime`), reached as `IPlatform::clock()` — the task offered
  "`AgentClock : IPlatform`'s clock half, or an adapter", and `AgentClock :
  IClock` is the former. `fromNetwork()` is what `linkProven()` read as
  `source() == Sntp || Server`; `sourceName()` is the one word `clock.sync`'s
  job log carries; `takeServerTime()` is what every config reply, heartbeat
  reply and claim offers the clock. `freeHeap()`/`minFreeHeap()` are
  implemented and unused until 18 (the heartbeat carries no heap today).
- **The log sink is a pointer, not a method of `IPlatform`.** `Log.h` gains
  `logSetSink(LogSink)`; unset, `logf` prints to `Serial` and the ring as
  before. `Log.cpp` therefore still includes `Arduino.h` and is not on this
  task's portable list — `test_buffers` holds the two lines
  `Serial.print(full); logRing().add(full, len);` in that file in that order,
  and this task may not change a test. 16 moves `Log` and that test together.
- **`ApplyExchange::nextTimestamp()` stays**, returning 0 on the board. The
  task said "goes the same way or disappears — read `ConfigApply` and decide":
  `test_config_apply`'s `FakeRepeater` overrides it and holds its series
  strictly increasing, so it cannot disappear without a test change; the
  engine still fills `ApplySend::timestamp` from it, and the comments on both
  say 0 means "the exchange draws it at the send".
- **`JobRunner::cliLine`'s sixth parameter is `uint32_t* timestampUsed`**

## Decisions taken without asking

- `kMeshReplyDataCap = 184` and `kMeshPathCap = 64` are MeshCore's at the pin
  written as the library's; `AgentMesh.h` asserts `>=` rather than `==`, so a
  MeshCore bump that *shrinks* either still builds and one that grows either
  is a build error there (the overrun would be in `takeResponse`/`onTraceRecv`).
- `extendLease(jobId, seconds, leaseUntil&)` and `postProgress(jobId, body,
  len)` are shaped like `claimJobs` and `postJobResult` respectively: the
  first writes its own one-field body and parses the reply into the new lease
  end; the second takes a body `writeProgressReport(dataJson, note, …)` made.
  The three per-job POSTs share one `postToJob()` and one 256-byte reply
  buffer (`g_jobReply`), so RAM did not grow by the two new statics it would
  have. A 409 (not the lease holder) is `false` with `http 409` logged, like
  any non-200.
- `writeProgressReport` leaves `data` and `note` out when nullptr, as every
  optional field of the envelopes is left out, and `{}` is a legal report.
- `IJobExtension::run` returns `false` to decline; the runner then logs *no
  handler for job type 'x' in this firmware or its extension* and reports
  `unsupported`. `JobContext` lends `mesh()`, `platform()`, `spend()`,
  `replyWaitMs()` and `log()`; `ResultWriter` takes `data(json, len)` (into
  the runner's data buffer, false when it does not fit) and `outcome(o)`. The
  extension gets no session: a login is its own, through `IMesh`.
- `kMaxExtensionTypes = 8`; the heartbeat's `jobTypes` is a member array of
  13 + 8 pointers, filled at each heartbeat, the thirteen first.
- `HeartbeatIdentity` carries `agentVersion`, `board`, `companionFirmware` and
  the `RadioParameters`; `transport` stays the loop's constant `"onboard"`.
  `main.cpp`'s `fillIdentity()` runs at boot, on every `set radio.*` and on
  `erase confirm` — the heartbeat read the settings live before, and these are
  the moments they change.
- The test of the two uplink calls is against the contract fixtures (the
  pattern of `test_claim`), plus greps of `AgentClient.cpp` for the two
  endpoints in `postToJob`'s shape and of the runner and the loop for *no*
  caller — the task's "FakeServer's pattern" names a fixture-driven test, and
  the fixtures are the only server this repository has.
- `test_seams` strips `//` comments before looking for MeshCore's identifiers,
  so `IMesh.h` may say in words what its constants stand for.
- `README.md` gained one sentence under *Building it*; `docs/` is unchanged
  because nothing the board prints, needs on the bench or is flashed with
  changed.

## Not verified here (and why)

The board on the bench, the next release; nothing should differ, which is the
point. In the order of `docs/console.md`'s walk:

1. **The heartbeat is byte-identical.** `set log.level debug`; the
   `server: heartbeat reply` line after the first heartbeat, and the exit
   node's page on meshmaster.dk showing the same `board`, `companionFirmware`,
   radio block and the thirteen `jobTypes` as under `v0.1.4` — the identity
   now comes from `fillIdentity()`, not from the settings at the moment of the
   heartbeat. Then `set radio.sf 9` (and back): the *next* heartbeat carries
   the new value.
2. **The login frame.** `set log.frames on`, then a `status` read from the
   server: the `frame tx anon-req flood hdr=.. path=- payload=..` line is the
   same shape as under `v0.1.4` — hex up to the key, then stars for a password
   login, whole for the ACL login — and the `frame rx response` that answers
   it. Paste one `frame tx` line into the next report: no report has one yet,
   and the next task that touches `logFrame` will want it.
3. **A CLI line's timestamp.** With `log.frames on`, an `acl.set` or a `cli`
   job: the `frame tx txt direct` line's first four payload bytes (the sender
   timestamp, little-endian) are strictly above the previous CLI line's, and
   a `config.apply` of a few keys prints each `set` exactly once with
   increasing timestamps, as 05's bench item says — the mesh draws them now.
4. **The console's four reports** (`jobs`, `sessions`, `server`, `errors`)
   print the same lines as before — they go through `TextOut` now — and
   `status`'s `server` line still says `root ISRG Root X1` (it is `via` now).
5. **The watchdog and the reboot guard.** `reboots` after a day shows nothing
   new; the heartbeat's `reboots` counter is `Esp32Platform::selfReboots()`,
   the same RTC count.
6. **Heap.** `status` after an hour of jobs: `free` and `min ever` flat
   against `v0.1.4` — RAM moved by 160 bytes, all of it `.bss`.

Nothing on the Heltec calls `extendLease` or `postProgress`; there is nothing
