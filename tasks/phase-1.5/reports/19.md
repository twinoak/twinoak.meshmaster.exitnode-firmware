# p1.5-19 — The hand-over

A second firmware can take this library now, and one document says what it gets:
[`docs/library.md`](../../../docs/library.md), some 750 lines for a reader who has
never opened this repository — what `agent-core` is and is not, **every method of
the three interfaces with its contract** (blocking or not, what a return value
means, what the runner does when it fails), the `MeshReply` constants a mesh
implementation must honour, `IJobExtension`, the lifecycle from `logBegin` to
`restore()`, the rails the consumer inherits with the test that holds each one
here, the fixtures as the wire contract, what a version bump may change, and the
first consumer's three implementations method by method against
`LINK-PROTOCOL.md` §6, API §3 and the ESP-IDF.

Nothing of the firmware changed. This task is `docs/library.md`, a *The library*
section in `README.md`, a new `CHANGELOG.md` written for a consumer, two
sentences in `docs/upstream.md` and seven follow-ups — no source file, no test,
no `platformio.ini`. **The version to tag is `v0.2.0`**, and §2 below has the
four size figures it is measured by.

Reading `docs/library.md` against the reader it is for found **five things the
library cannot do that a consumer is about to need**, every one of them now
written down rather than discovered on a bench: a mesh implementation cannot make
the runner say `unsupported`; the heartbeat's `transport` is the loop's constant
`"onboard"` where the next consumer must say `native`; the heartbeat always
advertises all thirteen job types, with no way to advertise fewer; a drop that
happened *before* the observation ring has nowhere to go in the envelope; and
`MeshReply::viaPath` and `snr` are filled by every implementation and read by
nobody. The first four are §7.1 of the page, the fifth is in its §3.1, and all of
them are items in `FOLLOWUPS.md`; §5 of this report says why none was fixed
here.

## 2. The verification, and the four numbers the tag is measured by

`pio test -e native` in the runbook's container: **356 cases in 22 suites, all
passing** (`out/19-native.log`) — the same 356 as `master`, because this task
changed no source and no test. CI's other three native steps ran in the same
container and are green: `tools/check-library-host.py` (*22 sources compiled with
-std=c++17 -Wall -Wextra -Werror -fno-exceptions -fno-rtti*) and
`cmake -P tools/check-idf-component.cmake` (*parses, and registers all 22
sources*). `tools/sync-fixtures.ps1 -Verify` ran on this machine: **47 contract
fixtures at `0595dc2` and 46 companion fixtures at `f518ebd`**, all matching
their manifests.

Both boards SUCCESS with `-Werror` (`out/19-boards.log`; the images are
`out/19-heltec_v3-firmware.bin` and `out/19-heltec_v4-firmware.bin`).

**The pile's exit criterion is "`pio run` for both boards is within 1 kB of
v0.1.4", and it is this task's to measure.** `v0.1.4` (`51faafb`) was built in a
worktree of its own on this machine with the same toolchain
(`out/19-v014-boards.log`):

| | RAM at v0.1.4 | RAM now | Flash at v0.1.4 | Flash now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| heltec_v3 | 160 072 | 160 280 (**+208**) | 1 359 209 | 1 350 621 (**−8 588**) |
| heltec_v4 | 160 044 | 160 252 (**+208**) | 1 356 681 | 1 348 185 (**−8 496**) |

**RAM is within 1 kB. Flash is not — it is 8.5 kB *smaller*** — and the two
figures are one fact, which `xtensa-esp32s3-elf-size -A` says in two lines
(`out/19-sections-v014.txt` against `out/19-sections-v020.txt`, V3):

| Section | v0.1.4 | now | |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `.dram0.data` | 51 192 | 34 296 | **−16 896** |
| `.dram0.bss` | 108 880 | 125 984 | **+17 104** |
| `.flash.text` | 900 191 | 905 663 | +5 472 |
| `.flash.rodata` | 334 796 | 337 632 | +2 836 |

Seventeen kilobytes of the agent's buffers — `g_jobs`, the two `ClaimedJob`s, and
`g_applyOutcome` — were *initialised* data on `v0.1.4`: their default member
values made the compiler store an image of them in flash and copy it into RAM at
boot. As members of task 15's one `JobRunner::Scratch` they are
zero-initialised instead, so 16.9 kB left the image and 17.1 kB arrived in
`.bss`; the +208 of RAM and the −8 588 of flash are both that, plus 8.3 kB of
real growth in code and read-only data for the three seams, the two new uplink
calls, the extension and the one-wake mode. **The four tasks' own figures add up
to exactly these totals**: −10 052 + 296 − 96 + 1 264 = **−8 588** on the V3 and
−9 848 + 156 − 84 + 1 280 = **−8 496** on the V4, which is the arithmetic of 15,
16, 17 and 18's reports in order. Every kilobyte of the growth is named by symbol
in those four reports.

So the criterion is met in RAM, missed in flash by being 8.5 kB smaller than
allowed, and the proposal's "to within the cost of three vtables" was never what
this pile's own deliverables cost — 15 said so in its first deviation and this
task's measurement against the release confirms it rather than correcting it.
**Nothing here grew unexplained**, which is what the criterion is for.

## 4. Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The changelog is a new `CHANGELOG.md`, not `docs/upstream.md`.** The task
  offered either and asked for a decision. `docs/upstream.md`'s *This repository
  is an upstream too* stays what it is — how pinning works — and gained two
  sentences pointing at the new file; the per-version record is
  [`CHANGELOG.md`](../../../CHANGELOG.md), at the root, written in a consumer's
  terms and starting at `v0.2.0` because the library did not exist before it. The
  reason is the upstream watch: `docs/upstream.md` moves whenever **MeshCore**
  moves, which would make a consumer's watch on it noisy for reasons that have
  nothing to do with the library, and the release notes
  `.github/workflows/release.yml` writes are about flashing a Heltec and are
  generated, not written. The release workflow is unchanged, as the task requires.
- **The size criterion is missed in flash and the report says how much and why**
  (§2) rather than treating it as a stop condition: the image did not grow
  unexplained, it shrank for a reason that is task 15's own deliverable, and the
  four tasks' figures sum to the total exactly. 15 raised this first and a
  reviewer may still disagree; nothing in this task can change it without undoing
  the scratch.
- **The page is longer than "one session, short" suggests.** The deliverable
  list asks for the three interfaces *method by method* with the contract of
  each, plus a lifecycle, a rails table, the fixtures, versioning and the
  consumer's mapping table — that is 700 lines however tersely it is written, and

- **`docs/console.md` and `docs/field.md` are unchanged**, as in 17 and 18:
  nothing the board prints, needs on the bench or is flashed with changed in this
  task. The bench walk of `docs/console.md` on `v0.2.0` is a follow-up, not a
  document change.
- **For the other repositories**: two items, both in `FOLLOWUPS.md`. The Walter's
  phase 4 tasks 02 and 04 each ask for something the library cannot do (§5), and
  that wording is theirs to change or ours to grow a field for. Nothing of
  proposal 03, `LINK-PROTOCOL.md` §6/§7 or API §3 disagreed with what is built.

## 5. Decisions taken without asking

- **The four limitations are documented and not fixed.** `transport` as a
  constant is one defaulted member of `HeartbeatIdentity` away, and it would
  change no byte of the Heltec's heartbeat and no test — I still did not make it.
  The pile's whole claim is that this board's bytes did not move; the task that
  writes the hand-over document is the wrong one to change the interface it is
  documenting, on the very commit that is about to be tagged as the first thing a
  consumer may pin; and the value a consumer needs (`native`) is meshmaster.dk's
  `AgentTransports` vocabulary, which nothing in this repository can validate.
  The same reasoning covers `MeshReply::viaPath` and `snr` (a struct change is a
  `CHANGELOG.md` line and a minor version), the thirteen advertised types (a loop
  change with a test of its own) and `unsupported` from a mesh (a runner change).
  All four are in `FOLLOWUPS.md` with what the fix would be, and all four are in
  `docs/library.md` §7.1 so that nobody writes a consumer against a promise the
  library does not make.
- **`v0.2.0` is the version to tag**, which is what `lib/agent-core/library.json`,
  `idf_component.yml` and `agent/Version.h` have said since task 16 and what this
  pile's `README.md` names as its exit criterion. The tree moved and the behaviour
  did not, so the minor goes up and the patch is 0. Nothing in this task touched
  those three files; `Library.TheManifestNamesTheLibraryItsVersionAndOneDependency`
  is what keeps them equal.
- **`CHANGELOG.md` starts at `v0.2.0`** with one paragraph for everything before
  it, rather than reconstructing five releases of firmware history for a reader
  who cannot pin any of them.
- **The rails table has six rows, not the four the task lists.** The task names
  four (nothing overheard logged, a password in RAM, one send per command, TLS
  verified); the two the README's own rails section adds and a consumer equally
  inherits are the path-hash width (D-33, and a rule of *the mesh
  implementation*, which is exactly the kind a hand-over loses) and the contract
  fixtures. Dropping them would have made the page's list disagree with
  `README.md`'s.
- **The page cites tests by their `TEST(...)` names**, taken from the files
  rather than from the reports, because a consumer's reviewer will grep for them.
  Nothing makes those names stable — a renamed test is a stale line in
  `docs/library.md` and no build will say so. The six suite *directories* are
  held by `Rails.TheTestsTheRailsLeanOnAreAllThere`, which is the coarse half of
  that and already existed.
- **`docs/library.md` is not held by a test.** `test_tls_policy` greps it for the
  ways of skipping certificate verification (it walks `docs/`), and
  `Rails.EveryPathTheRailsListNamesIsInTheTree` holds the paths in `README.md`'s
  rails section — but no test holds the page's method lists against the headers.
  A test that parsed the four headers and required every `virtual` in the page
  would be a real guard and is a task of its own; this session did that walk by
  hand and said so.

## 7. Not verified here (and why)

The whole of this task is a document, and a document's claims are verified by a
reader who has none of this repository's context — which no session is. In the
order the risk matters:

1. **The library consumed by the ESP-IDF** (the Walter's phase 4, task 01). The
   component file has been parsed and never compiled; the two things to expect
   are whether `bblanchon/arduinojson` 7.4.3 is on `components.espressif.com`
   under that exact name and whether an empty `REQUIRES` survives the component
   manager. `docs/library.md` §2 says it is unverified in as many words.
2. **Whether the page is enough**, which only the Walter's tasks 02–04 can
   answer. §3 is the best check a session can make — the page walked against
   those tasks' own deliverable bullets — and it is still a reading of a task
   file by the person who wrote the page. The first session to work from it
   should report what it had to open a header for; that is the measurement.
3. **The Heltec on the air at `v0.2.0`** — the bench walk of `docs/console.md`,
   now a follow-up with the three things this pile actually touched to watch (the
   four console reports through `TextOut`, the log's `[+123s]` stamp, and a CLI
   line's timestamp series) and the one thing three reports have asked for and
   none has: a `frame tx` line pasted into a report. **AarhusC and Grindsted are
   still on `v0.1.3`**, Risskov on `v0.1.4` (1.4's follow-ups), so `v0.2.0` is
   the image to flash them with and the guest-door checks 1.4 left go with it.
4. **The tag itself.** A release is a person pushing `v0.2.0` on a commit CI has
   passed; nothing in this task releases anything, and the workflow is untouched.
   What it will publish is per board a merged image and an application image with
   `SHA256SUMS`, and the notes it generates say nothing about the library — which
   is why `CHANGELOG.md` exists.
