# p1.9-37 — Measured, and released

Thomas walked 36's images on FD9B on 2 November and filled two of the pile
README's rows. This task puts those figures into `src/observer/Board.h`, decides
the V3/V4 rule from them rather than from 28 October's, writes them into
`docs/observer.md` and the release notes, and ships **v0.6.0**.

**The rule moved: both boards now open two broker slots.** That is what the rows
say, and the arithmetic is below. A V3 is no longer refused its MeshMapper slot.

## What the walk measured, and what follows from it

| | free at rest | min ever | largest block | the uplink's call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V3, no slot, ten minutes after boot | 120 392 | 93 988 | 102 388 | kept call lowest 113 988 (a dip of **6 404**); the one handshake, at boot, lowest 93 988 (a dip of **26 404**) — 1 handshake for 16 calls |
| V3, one MeshView slot, an hour | 100 924 | **69 736** | 86 004 | kept call lowest 94 712 (a dip of 6 212); no handshake in 55 calls; the slot's own handshake dipped to 74 960 |
| V4, two slots | — | — | — | not walked: Thomas had no V4 on 2 November |

So **one slot costs 19 468 bytes** (120 392 − 100 924), where on `v0.5.0` it cost
63 564, and **the uplink's handshake dips 26 404**, where it dipped 81 116. The
gate's sum (`Connection.h`: one slot + the uplink's dip + the floor + the margin)
is therefore 19 468 + 26 404 + 16 384 + 4 096 = **66 352** on the conservative
assumption of a handshake, and 46 352 once the board has measured a call over its
kept connection. The board has 120 392.

**Does a V3 fit two?** Yes, with room:

| | free | over the floor |
|---|---|---|
| two slots at rest | 120 392 − 2 × 19 468 = 81 456 | 65 072 |
| two slots and an uplink handshake | 81 456 − 26 404 = 55 052 | 38 668 |
| two slots and the hour's deepest trough (31 188, the slot's own handshake in it) | 81 456 − 31 188 = 50 268 | 33 884 |

The second slot's own gate check happens at 100 924 free, against a need of
66 352 — and two handshakes never overlap, because `Connection.cpp` holds the
second slot back while the first is in its handshake. On `v0.5.0` the same sum
was 136 580 − 63 564 − 81 116, which is below zero: that is why a V3's second
slot never left *waiting-for-heap*, and why it now does. The task's instruction —
*if a V3 fits two, say it and allow it* — is met, and the gate stays the live
guard: a board that turns out not to fit its second slot refuses to open it and
says so, and a call that comes near the floor still closes the highest slot for
ten minutes.

**`kHeapFloorBytes` does not move.** The hour with a slot up never came near it:
69 736 was the lowest the board ever saw, over four times the floor of 16 384,
and two slots would leave about 50 000. There is no measurement to raise it with
and none to lower it with — a floor that was never reached has not been tested —
so it stays at 16 384 with that sentence written beside it in `Connection.h`.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The V3 opens two slots, so the refusal's sentence is no longer any board's.**
  The task says *if a V3 fits two, say it and allow it*; the rows say it fits,
  so `slotRefusal`'s phrase changed from *this board has memory for one broker;
  a V4 carries two* to *this board has memory for one broker only*, and nothing
  this repository builds triggers it. The mechanism, the console refusal, the
  slot's `off (…)` line and the heartbeat's handling of a slot beyond the board
  are all untouched; only the sentence and the profiles' `slots` moved. Two tests
  bring a one-slot profile of their own so the path stays covered.
- **The V4's figures are the V3's unreduced, not derived by subtraction.** The
  task says *derived from the V3's measurement as 1.8/31 did (less what goes to
  PSRAM), conservatively*. 1.8/31 subtracted the two 16 KB content buffers
  because a connection *held* them; after 36 a connection holds neither between
  records, so the subtraction would now be of something that is not in the
  figure, and it would make the gate ask for **less** than the only measurement
  there is. Not subtracting is the conservative reading of the same instruction,
  and the source phrase says so in one line.
- **`docs/observer.md`'s static-RAM percentage** was task 31's 52.7 % and is this
  build's 57.8 %; it is a number in the section this task owns, and it was wrong
  on the page, so it moved with the rest. Nothing of the sentence's meaning
  changed.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **Two slots on a V3, from the arithmetic rather than from a second walk.** The
  hour that was walked had one slot. Two is the first table of this report: the
  worst figure the board ever showed with one slot up, less another slot, is
  50 268 — 33 884 over the floor — and the second slot's own gate check sees
  100 924 against a need of 66 352. The task delegates the decision to the rows,
  and the rows carry it with a margin of three floors. The risk if a board
  disagrees is bounded by the gate, which is why it was taken: a slot that does
  not fit waits instead of connecting, and a call that comes near the floor
  closes the highest slot. **A V3 with two slots up for an hour is on the bench
  list below** all the same.
- **The uplink's conservative dip is the handshake's 26 404, not the kept call's
  6 404.** A handshake was one call in sixteen and one in fifty-five on the two
  rows, so it is the rare case; but it is the expensive one, and the gate's
  constant is what stands in *before* a call has been measured on a boot. The
  board replaces it with its own first call either way.
- **`kHeapFloorBytes`, `kGateMarginBytes`, `kTlsBufferBytes` and
  `kPsramThresholdBytes` all stay.** The walk gives a figure for none of them:
  it never came near the floor, and the other three are properties of the TLS
  configuration, which 36 left at 16 KB in / 4 KB out and 4 KB for the PSRAM
  line. Their words moved; their values did not.
- **The release notes say the merged image's bootloader is new and untried.** The
  pile README makes that sentence conditional on the walk's optional step 4, and
  there is no record of it: the rows were read from a board flashed with the
  plain `.bin`. It names the symptom and the way back rather than warning in the
  abstract.
- **`## Unreleased` stays in `CHANGELOG.md` as *Nothing yet*** rather than being
  removed, which is the shape the file had before task 36 wrote into it.
- **The notes' *What's new* keeps the flash saving as a bullet** (≈58 KB), which
  is 36's measurement rather than this task's, because it is what a person
  flashing v0.6.0 sees in the file sizes.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot flash a board or read a heap; what follows is Thomas's with
`v0.6.0`'s images. The first two are this task's own, and neither is a blocker —
the gate is the guard for both.

1. **A V3 with two slots up for an hour.** `set mqtt1.preset meshview` and
   `set mqtt2.preset meshmapper` on FD9B, an hour beside the uplink with a job or
   two: `get mqtt.status`'s `memory` and both slots' lines, and `status`'s `heap`.
   What to read: **does slot 2 connect at all** (if it stays
   *waiting-for-heap*, its `last error` prints the sum and the heap, and the
   arithmetic of this report is wrong somewhere); **`min ever`** against the
   50 268 this report predicts; and **`gave way`** on either slot, which is a
   MeshMaster call that came near the floor with both up. A `gave way` climbing
   on a healthy board is the one reading that would send `kHeltecV3.slots` back
   to 1 — the figures are in one struct, and nothing else would have to move.
2. **The uplink's handshake with both slots up.** The expensive call is the rare
   one, so it may take a while to see: the row to fill is `status`'s `uplink`
   line, *lowest during*, on a call that was a handshake (the line counts
   handshakes against calls). This report's figure is 55 052; under about 20 000
   the floor is doing work and `kHeapFloorBytes` has a measurement at last.
3. **The web flasher's first install with the `-merged.bin`** — 36's bench item 1,
   still not done, which is why the notes carry the bootloader sentence. A spare
   board, not FD9B (a merged flash gives a new key): the board comes up, prints
   its banner at 115200, and `status` says what it said on `v0.5.0`. When that is
   walked, the notes' bullet can be edited out of the published release.
4. **A V4, two slots, an hour** — the pile README's row that stayed empty. Its
   figures in `Board.h` are the V3's and its PSRAM share is derived; the readings
   to take are `get mqtt.status`'s `memory` (its PSRAM clause), the `tls` line's
   *routed there* and *fell back* (a fallback is a PSRAM that could not serve a
   record's buffer), and `status`'s `psram free`.
5. **That nothing else changed.** No file under `src/` changed behaviour: the
   diff there is comments, the two profiles' numbers and one refusal string. The
   board should boot, print and publish exactly as 36's images did, and `status`
   should differ only in the version it reports and in the `memory` line's
   figures.
