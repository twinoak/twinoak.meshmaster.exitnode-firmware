# p1.8-31 — The observer fits

The observer's heap gate is now built from what this board was measured to cost
(`src/observer/Board.h`: the bench of 28 October 2026, per figure with its date,
or derived and saying so), not the observer firmware's 40 KB; it keeps watching
after a slot is open, reading what every MeshMaster call cost the heap through
task 30's record, and a call that dipped under a named floor closes the highest
slot for ten minutes exactly as a failed call did since task 27; a Heltec V3
opens **one** slot and refuses `mqtt2.preset` with the reason, a V4 opens
**two**; a V4 puts every large mbedtls allocation - the TLS content buffers of
the uplink and the brokers alike - in its PSRAM through the allocator hook task
30 installed, with no custom ESP-IDF build, and `get mqtt.status` counts both
heaps; the MQTT client's buffers and task stack are set with their arithmetic,
and the stack's low-water mark is read on the task so the bench can shrink it;
`docs/observer.md` has the per-board table.

**The README's row *after 30, no slot* had not been walked** when this task
ran, so the gate's constants are 28 October's (`v0.4.0`, a handshake per call),
and the report says so where it matters (below, *Deviations*).

## What changed

- **`src/observer/Board.{h,cpp}`** (new, host-portable): `BoardProfile` - name, slots, PSRAM, the slot's internal and PSRAM cost, the uplink's dip, a source phrase per figure - `kHeltecV3`, `kHeltecV4`, `boardProfile()`, `useBoardProfile()` for the tests, and `slotRefusal()`: the one phrase the console's refusal, the slot's `off (…)` line and the log all print.
- **`src/observer/Connection.{h,cpp}`**: `kTlsConnectionBytes`, `kUplinkReserveBytes`, `kHeapReserveFree` and `kHeapReserveLargest` are gone; the gate is `gateNeedBytes()` = the board's slot cost + the uplink's dip (measured on this boot through `onUplinkCall()`, else the board's constant) + `kHeapFloorBytes` + `kGateMarginBytes`, the largest internal block one content buffer on a V3 or `kPsramThresholdBytes` on a V4, and on a V4 PSRAM's free and largest block as well (`Environment.freePsram`, `largestPsram`, `board`). `onUplinkCall(freeBefore, lowest, freeAfter)` records the dip and yields the highest slot when `lowest < kHeapFloorBytes`; `onUplinkMemoryFailure()` and it share `yieldHighest()`. `configure()` turns a slot beyond the board's into `off` with the refusal when a preset is stored in it. `describeGate()` is the `memory` line.
- **`src/platform/TlsHeap.{h,cpp}`** (new, board-only): the wrapper of mbedtls's allocator that task 30 put in `AgentClient.cpp`, moved here because it now serves both ends: the watch (`watchBegin`, `sample`, `watchEnd`) as before, and on `BOARD_HAS_PSRAM` the routing of every allocation of `kPsramFromBytes` (4 096) and over to `heap_caps_calloc(MALLOC_CAP_SPIRAM | MALLOC_CAP_8BIT)` with the internal heap as the fallback, counted (`psramRouted`, `psramFallbacks`). `AgentClient.cpp` calls it; its `HeapCost` is unchanged.
- **`src/net/MqttClient.{h,cpp}`**: the three sizes with their arithmetic (above), `kTaskStackBytes`, `stackLowWater()`.
- **`src/settings/Keys.cpp`**: `validateValue` refuses a preset other than `none` for an `mqttN.preset` the board has no slot for, with `slotRefusal()`'s phrase.
- **`src/observer/Observer.{h,cpp}`**: `onUplinkCall()` through; `slotsOnBoard()`, `slotShown()` (the board's own slots, plus a refused one with something stored in it); `describe()` and the heartbeat's `slotCount` use them, so a V3's heartbeat carries one slot; a refused slot does not turn the observer on by itself.
- **`src/main.cpp`**: `handUplinkToObserver()` - the memory-failure hand-over of task 27 and the new per-call one (`KeepAlive`'s call count moved → `AgentClient::lastCost()`), from the main loop and from the job pump alike; the PSRAM readings in the `Environment`; `get mqtt.status`'s `memory`, `uplink`, `tls` and `stack` lines; `status`'s `heap` line gains `psram free N, largest block N` on a V4.
- **`src/console/Console.cpp`**: `help`'s line for `get mqtt.status`.
- **`test/test_connection`**: the rig takes a `BoardProfile` (the V4's by default, so the two-slot tests of pile 1.7 run as written) and fakes PSRAM; the heap test uses `gateNeedBytes()` and holds the largest-block rule on both boards; four new tests (above). **`test/test_console_parser`**: one new test; the existing `set mqtt2.preset custom` line notes the host's profile.
- **`platformio.ini`**: `observer/Board.cpp` in the native filter.
- **`docs/observer.md`**: *The lines to type* (the V3's refusal), *What it costs* (the table and the rules), the `get mqtt.status` sample with the four new lines and a V3's reading, the field table. **`docs/console.md`**: the `get mqtt.status` row. **`README.md`**: the two `status` samples show one slot, as a V3 prints now.

**Read against the framework it builds against** (arduino-esp32 `3.20017.241212`, its `tools/sdk/esp32s3/qio_qspi/include/sdkconfig.h`, `cores/esp32/Esp.cpp` and `esp32-hal-psram.c`, `soc_caps.h`, esp-mqtt's `mqtt_client.h`), because the host test fakes the board and these are the things the code assumes:

- `ESP.getFreeHeap()`, `getMinFreeHeap()`, `getMaxAllocHeap()` are `MALLOC_CAP_INTERNAL`; `getFreePsram()`, `getMaxAllocPsram()` are `MALLOC_CAP_SPIRAM` and 0 when `psramFound()` is false. So task 30's `HeapCost` and the gate's `free` are internal heap on both boards, which is what the floor is about.
- The core's sdkconfig has `CONFIG_SPIRAM_USE_MALLOC` with `CONFIG_SPIRAM_MALLOC_ALWAYSINTERNAL 4096` and no `CONFIG_SPIRAM_BOOT_INIT`: `initArduino()` calls `psramInit()`, which on a V4 initialises the PSRAM, adds it to the heap and enables the 4 KB malloc policy, and on a V3 fails quietly (no chip) and leaves malloc internal. The same image rule serves both; `BOARD_HAS_PSRAM` only chooses the profile and compiles the routing in.
- mbedtls is built with `CONFIG_MBEDTLS_INTERNAL_MEM_ALLOC`: its pair is `heap_caps_calloc(MALLOC_CAP_INTERNAL)`, so without the hook its buffers never reach PSRAM however the core's malloc is set. `CONFIG_MBEDTLS_EXTERNAL_MEM_ALLOC` is the ESP-IDF's own way of putting all of mbedtls there, which is the configuration this hook reproduces for the large allocations.
- `SOC_PSRAM_DMA_CAPABLE`, `SOC_AES_GDMA`, `SOC_SHA_GDMA` are set for the S3, and `CONFIG_MBEDTLS_HARDWARE_AES/SHA/MPI` are on: the AES and SHA engines reach a content buffer in PSRAM through their DMA. That is what makes the routing possible at all; whether it is fast enough and clean on this core is the bench's (below).
- esp-mqtt at this pin names the sizes `buffer_size`, `out_buffer_size`, `task_stack` (the task's `buffer.size`, `buffer.out_size`, `task.stack_size` are ESP-IDF 5's names). `uxTaskGetStackHighWaterMark` returns bytes on this port (`portSTACK_TYPE` is `uint8_t`).

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The README's *after 30, no slot* row is not there, so the constants are 28 October's**, as the README said they would be. The uplink's dip of 81 116 is a handshake per call, which is more than a call over task 30's kept connection dips - and with it alone the V3's gate would ask for 165 KB, more than the board has, and never open. That is why the gate **takes the dip from the board's own last call** once there has been one (`onUplinkCall`, the "every uplink call reads 30's lowest free heap" of deliverable 3, used for the opening gate as well as the watching one): the constant is the conservative figure until the board has measured itself, and `get mqtt.status` says `assumed` or `measured`. The last call is the predictor, not the worst since boot: the worst since boot is the first handshake at boot, which would keep a V3 shut for ever; the last call follows the kept connection's steady cost, and a handshake that arrives while a slot is up is exactly the case the watching gate and the yield exist for. When Thomas pastes the row, `kHeltecV3.uplinkDipBytes` is one line.
- **The task's "`src/net/MqttClient.cpp` … sets no buffer or task size today"**: it set the two buffers (512, 1 536) since task 27 and not the stack. The send buffer **grew**, from 1 536 to 1 792: the arithmetic says the CONNECT with a 768-byte token, a 768-byte will and a 128-byte topic is 1 780 bytes, which 1 536 could not hold whole, and a message that does not fit esp-mqtt's out buffer is a failed CONNECT. (On the bench the token and the will are shorter, which is why task 27's figure worked.) The receive buffer stays at 512 with the reason written beside it: esp-mqtt's source is not on disk at this pin to prove a smaller floor against, and the gain would be a quarter kilobyte. So "smaller slots" is a smaller *count* on a V3 and smaller *internal* slots on a V4, not smaller buffers: what dominates a slot is mbedtls's 33 KB, which cannot shrink without the custom build the task puts out of scope.
- **The task stack is set to esp-mqtt's default (6 144), not smaller.** What the handshake needs on that task is the one figure that can only be measured on the task, so this task adds the measurement (`stackLowWater`, on `get mqtt.status`) and leaves the cut to the bench walk: a stack overflow is a crash, and 1 KB is not worth guessing at.
- **`kQueueBytes` stays** (deliverable 1's "stays or shrinks with a reason"): the queue is static `.bss`, not heap, so shrinking it would free nothing the gate counts.
- **The V4's constants are derived, not measured**, and say so in `Board.h` and in the table. A V4 was not on the 28 October bench.
- **A V3's heartbeat carries one slot** (`slotCount` is the board's), not two with the second `off`: MeshMaster's card would otherwise show *no broker* for a slot the board cannot have. The server takes one to six slots (meshmaster.dk `AgentObserverRules`); `test_envelopes`'s fixtures are the library's own and are unchanged.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **Where the per-board profile lives: `src/observer/Board.h`**, beside the gate it feeds, rather than `src/platform/` (board-only) or `src/version.cpp` (a string). It is host-portable because the tests must run both boards' rules on the host; `Keys.cpp` includes it for the console's refusal the way it already includes `observer/Presets.h`.
- **The host's default profile is the V4's** (`Board.cpp` says why): the tests of pile 1.7 were written for two slots, and running them as written is worth more than the host "being" a V3. A test of the V3's rule switches with `useBoardProfile(&kHeltecV3)` or `Rig(kHeltecV3)` and says so.
- **The refusal is the preset's alone.** `set mqtt2.server …`, `mqtt2.audience`, `mqtt2.topic`, `mqtt2.username`, `mqtt2.password` are still stored on a V3: they are harmless, they are the Danish guide's `custom` lines, and a V3 that takes them and refuses `mqtt2.preset` with one line ends the guide in a valid configuration with one `error:` that says why. The refusal's words are one constant (`slotRefusal`), so the console, `get mqtt.status` and the log cannot drift apart.
- **The watching gate yields on `lowest < floor`, not on `lowest − slotCost < floor`**: the slot is already open when the call is made, so what the call saw is what matters.
- **A refused slot that has a preset stored is shown, not hidden**: `get mqtt.status` on a V3 prints slot 1, and slot 2 only if something is stored in it, as `off (this board has memory for one broker; a V4 carries two)`. The task's "`get mqtt.status` shows one slot" is true of a V3 that was never given a second.
- **The allocator wrapper moved from `AgentClient.cpp` to `src/platform/TlsHeap.cpp`** because it now has two owners (the uplink's watch, the observer's PSRAM). `test_library`'s list of `src/agent/` is unchanged.
- **The PSRAM threshold is the core's own 4 096**, not "everything": the contexts, keys and bignum limbs stay internal, which is where the hardware MPI and the cache want them, and it is the line the core already draws for every other malloc on the board. The two content buffers and the handshake's larger transients are what move.
- **The `tls` line prints fallbacks**: a V4 whose PSRAM allocation fails would silently put a 16 KB buffer in internal heap and the gate would be wrong about it; a count on `get mqtt.status` makes that visible.
- **`handUplinkToObserver()` runs from the job pump too**, because a heartbeat made inside a thirteen-minute job is a call whose dip the gate should read then, not when the job ends.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot flash a board, reach a broker or read a heap. On the bench,
with this build's images (both are in CI's artifacts and `out/`):

1. **A V3, one slot.** Flash, let the uplink make its first calls, then `get
   mqtt.status`: the `memory` line should read `heltec_v3 opens 1 slot(s) while
   free heap >= N (one slot 63564 + the uplink's dip D measured + floor 16384 +
   margin 4096) …`. After a reused call `D` should be a few KB and the slot
   `connecting` then `connected`; right after a handshake (`uplink: handshake`
   on the log) `D` is tens of KB and the slot may wait five seconds. **Paste the
   `memory` line, `status`'s `heap` and `uplink` lines into the README's
   *Measured* table as the row *after 31, one slot*** - `free` at rest with the
   slot up, `min ever`, and the uplink's `lowest during` of a reused call and
   of a handshake call. `kHeltecV3.slotInternalBytes` and `.uplinkDipBytes` are
   what those move.
2. **The V3's handshake with the slot up** is the case this task could not
   settle from the desk: 55 464 min-ever with no slot (28 October) less a 63 564
   slot is under zero, and the 28 October call went through at 3 088 free. Leave
   the board an hour; when the server closes the idle connection (about every
   two minutes of idling, or after a job), the next call handshakes. Three
   outcomes, each readable: the call goes through above the floor (`gave way 0`,
   the slot stays - one slot on a V3 fits); it goes through under the floor
   (`observer: slot 1 meshview: closed for 10 min, the MeshMaster call dipped to
   N free, under the floor of 16384`, `gave way` counts, the slot is back ten
   minutes later - the V3 is marginal, and `kHeapFloorBytes` is the constant to
   discuss); or it fails for want of memory (`server: the call failed for want
   of memory`, the same yield, the loop's retry goes through - the V3 does not
   fit, and the custom ESP-IDF build is the next pile). `min ever` on `status`
   is the one number to write down.
3. **A V3's refusal.** `set mqtt2.preset meshmapper` answers `error:
   mqtt2.preset: this board has memory for one broker; a V4 carries two`; `set
   mqtt2.preset none` is taken. A V3 still carrying `v0.4.0`'s slot 2 shows
   `slot 2 meshmapper: off (this board has memory for one broker; a V4 carries
   two)` on `get mqtt.status` and the same on the log at boot; `status`'s
   `observer` line names one slot; the OLED reads `MQTT 1/1`; the exit node's
   page shows one row.
4. **A V4, two slots, the TLS buffers in PSRAM.** `status`'s `heap` line gains
   `psram free N, largest block N`; `get mqtt.status`'s `tls` line counts
   allocations `routed there` climbing with every handshake (about four per
   handshake: two content buffers and the handshake's transients) and `0 fell
   back`; `memory` says `heltec_v4 opens 2 slot(s)` and the `psram >= 40960`
   clause; both slots connect; a heartbeat's `lowest during` is far above the
   V3's. **This is the one thing this task did that could fail on hardware
   alone**: a TLS handshake whose content buffers are in PSRAM, with the S3's
   AES and SHA engines reading them over DMA. A V4 whose handshakes fail with
   `tls handshake failed` or whose uplink calls fail while a V3 with the same
   settings works is that; the routing is `src/platform/TlsHeap.cpp`'s
   `wrappedCalloc`, and raising `kPsramFromBytes` above 17 KB turns it off.
5. **The stack.** After an hour, `get mqtt.status`'s `stack` lines: `the least
   ever free N`. If N is above 2 048 on both boards through a handshake and a
   renewal, `MqttClient::kTaskStackBytes` can come down by a kilobyte in a
   follow-up; if it is under 512, it must go up, and that is a finding for
   FOLLOWUPS.md.
6. **The send buffer.** A slot that connects proves the CONNECT fits (1 792); a
   `last error` naming a CONNECT that could not be made would be the arithmetic
   being wrong, and is not expected.
7. **What task 27's walk asked for still stands** (its report's list: the
   renewal a day later, the will on a `reboot`, `erase confirm`), unchanged by
   this task.
