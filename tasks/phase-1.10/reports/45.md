# p1.10-45 — The owner info, whole

`REQ_TYPE_GET_OWNER_INFO` answers three lines — `FIRMWARE_VERSION`, the node
name, the owner info — and this library kept the first and threw the other two
away, at class `acl` alone. All three are kept now, and the request goes at
**every** class a `status` read goes at, which is meshmaster.dk's p4.30-181
contract: that task shows *Owner info* and *Name on the radio* on every repeater
page with an association of any class, and it is this reply they come from.
`handleRequest` was read at the pin rather than quoted —
`sprintf((char *) &reply_data[4], "%s\n%s\n%s", FIRMWARE_VERSION,
_prefs.node_name, _prefs.owner_info)` with **no permission check at all** on that
branch — and so were the two widths: `node_name[32]` and `owner_info[120]` of
`src/helpers/CommonCLI.h`, and `FIRMWARE_VER_LEVEL 2` of
`examples/simple_repeater/MyMesh.cpp:44`, the level the request's own comment
names.

**First:** `master` of meshmaster.dk has `tasks/phase-4.30/reports/181.md`
(13 192 bytes), so this task ran rather than stopping.

## Deliverables, one by one

1. **When.** `readsFirmwareVersion(job)` became **`readsOwnerInfo(job)`** and
   lost its class clause: the claim's `StatusPayload.version` is the whole of the
   answer, exactly as `StatusPayload.ReadsOwnerInfo(bool?)` is on the server, and
   absent still means *read it*. The field keeps the name `version` on the wire,
   for 181's reason. The **other half of *when*** is the firmware level:
   `JobRunner::Context` gained `hasFirmwareLevel` / `firmwareLevel`, filled by
   `loginOnce` from the login reply's `FIRMWARE_VER_LEVEL` byte, and a level
   **below `kOwnerInfoVerLevel` (2)** skips the request with one log line saying
   why. Not knowing is never a reason to skip — a job that reused an earlier
   job's session was told no level, and it asks. A local job (tasks 40, 41) runs
   the same rule and spends no airtime on it
   (`RunnerTest.ALocalJobAsksForTheOwnerInfoOnTheSameRuleAndSpendsNoAirtime`: one
   owner-info request, `transactions: 0`).
2. **All of it.** `parseOwnerInfoFirmware` became
   **`parseOwnerInfo(data, len, OwnerInfoReply&)`**, which drops the cipher
   block's trailing NULs (the .NET agent's `TrimEnd('\0')`) and splits at the
   **first two** newlines and no further, so the owner info is the rest of the
   reply verbatim with its own newlines kept. `writeStatusResult` takes that
   struct in place of its `const char* firmware` and writes `firmware`,
   `nodeName` and `ownerInfo` in the record's own order, JSON-escaped by the one
   writer (a newline travels as `\n`), bounded at **31** and **119** — the
   firmware's own fields — and **absent** when the request was not made. A cut
   value says so in the job's log.
3. **Tests.** Above. The fake companion grew the two knobs 181 gave its own
   (`setFirmwareVerLevel`, which also makes the fake answer the request with the
   silence a repeater below level 2 gives, so the skip is worth something; and
   `setOwnerInfoReply` for a reply that is not the fixture's three lines).
4. **The price.** `transactionsFor("status", class)` is **2 at every class**, as
   `TransactionCosts.StatusRequests()` is since 181 (it lost its class parameter
   there for the same reason), and `transactionsOf` still takes it back to 1 for
   a claim that said the reply is not wanted. Not in the deliverables; see the
   deviations for why it is here.
5. **The words.** `docs/console.md`'s paragraph on the status read's moving
   transaction count (the request is `owner info` now, not `owner info
   (firmware)`; at any class; the three fields; the level-1 skip line), and
   `CHANGELOG.md`'s *Unreleased* — three free functions of the library changed
   shape and the fixture manifest moved, which is exactly what a consumer that
   pins this repository has to be told.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **An empty owner info is `""`, not absent** — the task's fifth test bullet says
  the other way round, and the spec wins. API §3 says *all three are absent on a
  read that did not make the request … and absent stores nothing*, and nothing
  more; 181's own report decides it in as many words (*An empty reading is
  stored … because that is the difference between the owner has written nothing
  and nobody has asked. Only an **absent** field stores nothing*), its page has
  the sentence *Read, and empty* for exactly that case, and
  `Handlers.cs` writes it: `Split('\n', 3)` with
  `lines.Length > 2 ? Bounded(…) : null`, so a third line that is **empty** is
  `""` and a third line that is **not there** is `null`. The firmware cannot send
  the task's version of it either — its `sprintf` writes both newlines whatever
  the two fields hold, so an unset `owner.info` *is* an empty third line. Doing
  what the task bullet says would have left a repeater whose owner wrote nothing
  reading *Not read yet* on meshmaster.dk for ever. **What is absent** is a line
  the reply did not carry at all, and that is tested beside the empty one.
- **`transactionsFor("status", …)` is 2 at every class**, which no deliverable
  asks for. The owner-info request now goes at guest and admin as well, and
  `AgentLoop.cpp`'s own rule for this number is that *an estimate a battery
  spends against may be too high and must never be too low*: left at 1 below
  `acl`, a guest-class status read would have been paced and budgeted for one
  transaction and spent two. `PlannedLoad.cs` made the same change upstream.
  `test_airtime`'s two status cases and the status+telemetry pair moved with it
  (the guest pair is four transactions now, not three).
- **One existing reply-codec test was rewritten rather than kept.**
  `OwnerInfo.OnlyTheFirmwareVersionIsKeptAndTheNameAndOwnerAreNot` asserted the
  behaviour this task exists to change; it is now
  `OwnerInfo.TheThreeLinesOfTheReplyAreAllKept` over the same fixture bytes. Two
  call sites in `test_job_results` moved with `writeStatusResult`'s fourth
  parameter, and one was renamed
  (`AnAclReadCarriesTheFirmwareTheOwnerInfoRequestFetched` →
  `AReadCarriesAllThreeLinesTheOwnerInfoRequestFetched`, since the class no
  longer decides). No assertion about behaviour that did not change was touched.
- **The fixture sync brought one file this task does not use.**
  `tests/fixtures/contracts` is copied whole at one commit, and meshmaster.dk's
  **180 merged before 181** (17:32 and 19:14 on 11 November), so there is no
  commit carrying 181's `StatusResult.json` without 180's
  `JobResultReportAtGuest.json`. That new fixture is task 46's and nothing here
  reads it; it is listed in `MANIFEST.txt` and well formed, which is all
  `test_contract_fixtures` asks of it. Contracts are now at `cd872a7`. The
  **companion** set did not move and was not re-synced.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The level lives on the job's context, not on the cached session.** 181 took
  the same decision for the same reason (`JobContext.FirmwareVerLevel`, and
  putting it on `RepeaterSession` *is not in the deliverables*). So a job that
  reused a session knows no level and asks; in practice a `status` is the first
  job of its group and makes the login itself, so a repeater too old for the
  request costs at most one timeout a day in the arrangement where it does not.
- **An empty *version* line is still left out, while an empty name or owner info
  is written.** `FIRMWARE_VERSION` is a compile-time `#define`, so a blank first
  line is no firmware's reply; `firmware: ""` is not a version the server could
  show, and omitting it is what this writer already did. The two fields 181 added
  are a person's text, where empty is an answer.
- **A reply that is nothing but padding is *not read* rather than
  `firmware: ""`.** `parseOwnerInfo` answers false and the job logs *the owner
  info was not read; the status itself stands*, which is what the old parser did
  with a zero-length reply and what a reader of the result needs: a request that
  came back empty is not a repeater that told us three empty things.
- **A cut is in the log and not in the result.** 181's half does the same — the
  value is kept, short, and `Bounded` warns — so the server stores what arrived
  and the walk has the sentence.
- **The request's log label is `owner info`**, not `owner info (firmware)`: the
  reply is three facts now, and that is the .NET agent's own word for it.
  `docs/console.md` moved with it.
- **The two cut cases are two reads in the test, not one.** `node_name[32]` and
  `owner_info[120]` bound a real reply at 159 bytes and
  `Mesh::createDatagram` refuses a plaintext past 167, so a single reply with
  both fields over-long is one no mesh can carry — the fake refuses it exactly as
  the firmware would, which is how this was found.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session can build, run the host tests and read the MeshCore source it builds
against. It cannot flash a board or hear a radio, so this list is Thomas's with
the board on the bench. Every item is readable off the serial console (`jobs`)
and off the job's page on meshmaster.dk.

1. **A real repeater's three lines.** An `acl`-class *Read the status* of a
   repeater whose owner info is a day old: `jobs` should show **2
   transaction(s)** and the job's log the `owner info` request, and the
   repeater's page should fill *Name on the radio* and *Owner info* under *Key
   facts*. The second read of the same day should be **1** and say nothing about
   the owner info.
2. **The same at guest.** The one thing only the bench can show: a **guest**-door
   pair, on somebody else's repeater, filling both rows. This is the change's
   whole point and it has never gone out on the air at that class. Watch for an
   `unauthorized` or a silence instead — the firmware was read as checking no
   permission for this request, and the bench is where that reading is tested.
3. **An owner info of two lines.** `set owner.info TwinOak|thomas@twinoak.dk`
   through a `cli` job, then a status read: the page should show two lines
   (`whitespace-pre-line`), not a `|`.
4. **An empty owner info.** `set owner.info ` with nothing after it, then a read:
   the page should say *Read, and empty* rather than *Not read yet*. That is the
   deviation above, end to end.
5. **A repeater on firmware older than v1.12.** Its login reply says
   `FIRMWARE_VER_LEVEL` 1 (or carries no level byte at all, which is the legacy
   `OK` reply): the job's log should carry *owner info not asked for: the login
   said firmware level 1, and the request arrived at level 2*, `jobs` should show
   **1 transaction** for the read, and the job must not wait out a reply timeout.
   No such repeater is on this mesh, so this is the one item that may need one
   downgraded on purpose — or skipping.
6. **A value longer than the firmware's field.** Unreachable on stock firmware
   (the two array sizes make it so), so there is nothing to walk; the host test
   stands in for it and the log line is what to look for if it ever appears.
7. **The Walter's local door.** `LinkMesh::localLogin` answers at admin whatever
   the login carried; whether its reply carries a `FIRMWARE_VER_LEVEL` byte is
   the Walter's business and not readable from here. If it carries none, a local
   job asks for the owner info (not knowing is not a reason to skip), which is
   the intended behaviour and costs the local door nothing — but it is worth one
   look at a unit's own repeater's page after its next agent-core sync, that both
   rows fill.
