# p1.10-47 — Only a login reply answers a login

A login reply starts with the **repeater's** clock and carries no tag of ours, so
a login is awaited with tag 0 — *the next response from this peer* — and whatever
came next was taken as its answer. On a job whose request had just timed out,
what comes next is that request's **late** reply; it did not parse as a login
reply; the runner said *refused*; the job ended `unauthorized` with no data. That
is how meshmaster.dk parked ten working ACL associations as *the ACL entry is
gone* on 12 November 2026 (jobs 24142, 21783, 8293, 19065 — in 24142 the same key
had been answered *logged in as read-write* three minutes before).

`JobRunner::awaitLoginReply` is now the whole of a login's wait, and the only one
there is: `loginOnce`, the reachability probe's blank login and the discover's two
logins all go through it. A response that is not a login reply is **dropped**,
one line goes into the job's log, and the wait runs to its end — then it is *no
reply*, which is what stock firmware answers a login it will not take anyway, and
the second try follows as for any silence.

## Deliverables, one by one

1. **While a login is awaited, only a login reply ends the wait.**
   `JobRunner::awaitLoginReply(job, ctx, what, LoginReply&)` holds the deadline
   (`loginWaitMs(job)`, unchanged) and loops: `awaitReply(0, what is left, pump_)`,
   and on a reply that is not a login reply one `g_log.add` line, one `LOG_I` on
   the serial console, and round again with the remainder. False — *no reply* — is
   the deadline passing, which `loginOnce` reports exactly as it reported a silence
   before. The drops do **not** extend the wait, and there is a bound of eight per
   wait: eight late replies while one login is awaited is a repeater talking about
   something else, and the login was not answered.
2. **A late reply is known by its tag.** `Context::rememberTag` / `Context::sentTag`,
   a ring of the last eight tags this job's **requests** went out with, filled in
   `exchange()` for every non-text attempt and for the discover's own
   `sendDiscovery`. A CLI line is not one of them and the comment says why: its
   reply is a `TXT_MSG` that carries no tag at all, and the timestamp it drew is
   the repeater's replay mark, never a response's four bytes. The tag is tested
   **before** the parse, because the reply the parse cannot catch is caught by
   nothing else.
3. **In the library, not only in the Heltec's mesh.** `AgentMesh` is unchanged
   except for two comments, which now say why it hands *every* response through on
   a tag-0 wait: the rule has to hold for an `IMesh` that filters nothing, and the
   Walter's does. Nothing is asked of an implementor — `docs/library.md` §3.6 ends
   with *For your `IMesh`: do nothing*.
4. **Nothing says *refused* of a login the repeater did not answer.** The parse
   branch of `loginOnce` is gone; `probeReachable` has no refusal arm; the
   discover's `learned()` still acts on `LoginResult::Refused`, which now only ever
   means *answered below the class*. The word is gone from the types as well, not
   merely made unreachable — see the deviations.
5. **`FakeMesh` can be late.** Above. `Pending` gained `sendIndex` and
   `waitsToSkip`, `late_` is the list of replies still on their way home, and
   `endJob()` clears it because a reply still in flight belongs to a job that is
   over. One fix came with it: `Send::answered` is now set on the send the reply
   **belongs to** rather than on the last send, which a late delivery would
   otherwise have credited to the login that followed it.
6. **The words.** Above.

## Where I looked for a refusal, and what one would look like

The task asks not to keep the word unproven. At the pin (`81dbae59`, on disk under
`.pio/libdeps/heltec_v3/MeshCore`):

- `examples/simple_repeater/MyMesh.cpp`, `handleLoginReq` (90–145): a blank
  password and no ACL entry falls through to the password comparison; a password
  that is neither `_prefs.password` nor `_prefs.guest_password` is `return 0`; a
  sender timestamp at or below the client's last is `return 0`; everything else is
  thirteen bytes beginning `RESP_SERVER_LOGIN_OK`. **Silence or an answer, never a
  refusal.**
- `examples/simple_room_server/MyMesh.cpp` (~360–395) and
  `examples/simple_sensor/SensorMesh.cpp` (~385–415), the other two servers that
  answer a login: the same thirteen bytes, or nothing.
- `grep -rn 'RESP_SERVER_LOGIN|LOGIN_FAIL|PUSH_CODE_LOGIN'` over the whole pinned
  tree: `RESP_SERVER_LOGIN_OK` is **0** and is the only login response code
  defined anywhere; the only `..._FAIL` is the **companion's**
  `PUSH_CODE_LOGIN_FAIL` (`examples/companion_radio/MyMesh.cpp:123`), which
  `onContactResponse` (716–721) emits for a response that is neither the legacy
  `"OK"` nor `RESP_SERVER_LOGIN_OK` — that is, for exactly the reply this task
  drops. The USB companion makes the same mistake this firmware made, which is
  what meshmaster.dk's pile 4.33 task 196 is for on the .NET side.

So a refusal would have to be a `RESPONSE` whose first byte after the tag is some
`RESP_SERVER_LOGIN_FAIL` code, and no such code exists at the pin; the only other
login reply shape is the legacy two-byte `"OK"`, which `parseLoginReply` still
accepts and which this change does not touch. `unauthorized` **from a refusal**
remains in the protocol, as EXIT-NODES §7 says it does — the server reads it, and
`LoginSilentResult.probeAnsweredAs` still admits the string `refused` for an agent
whose companion emits that frame. This firmware simply never writes it.

## Can a status reply parse as a login reply?

The task asks. **Yes**, one reading in 256. `RepeaterStats` begins
`uint16_t batt_milli_volts`, little-endian, so a battery that is a multiple of 256
(3840, 4096, 4352 mV) puts a zero in `data[0]` and `parseLoginReply` takes it for
`RESP_SERVER_LOGIN_OK`. What it then reads is nonsense: `data[2..3]` is
`curr_tx_queue_len`, so the permission byte is that counter's **high** byte — 0,
i.e. *guest*, for any queue below 256 — and `data[8]` is the first byte of
`n_packets_recv`, read as the `FIRMWARE_VER_LEVEL`. The status fixture's own 4012
mV (`0x0FAC`) does not, which is why the first test's late status reply is dropped
by its **tag** and not by the parse — and why the tag half of the rule is what
that test proves.

Two notes beside it:

- The **empty neighbour page is worse than the task says.** Four zero bytes after
  the tag, yes — but `Utils::encrypt` zero-pads the plaintext to the cipher block,
  so twelve arrive, which is past `parseLoginReply`'s `len >= 9` and makes it read
  a **firmware level of 0** as well. A level below `kOwnerInfoVerLevel` suppresses
  the owner-info request (task p1.10-45), so that one page used to be able to say
  *logged in as guest* **and** *too old for the owner info* at once.
- The **access-list reply is the worst of the three**: its body is
  `{pub_key[6], permissions}` per entry, so `data[0]` is a key byte (zero one time
  in 256) and `data[3]` is another, whose low two bits read as any of the four
  roles — *admin* included. It only ever arrives inside a `config.read`, whose
  class is admin, and a late one now goes the same way as the rest: dropped by its
  tag.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **`tools\verify.ps1` does not exist in this repository.** The last acceptance
  criterion names it (so do tasks 48, 49 and 50), and there is no such script in
  `tools/`; what it stands for is run instead, and by hand: the native suite in a
  container, `pio run -e heltec_v3`, and the four other steps of CI's native job
  (`check-runners.py`, `check-library-host.py`, `check-idf-component.cmake`,
  `sync-fixtures.ps1 -Verify`). The rails themselves — verified TLS, no
  `setInsecure`, no secret in a log line — are native tests (`test_tls_policy`,
  `test_seams`, `test_credentials`) and are in the 593. Either the script should be
  written or the three remaining task files should say what this one says; that is
  the reviewer's call and it is not this task's scope.
- **The word was removed from the types, not only from the branches.** Deliverable
  4 asks that a response that does not parse is never *refused*, and *do not keep
  the word for it unproven*. Making the branch unreachable would have left
  `mm::ProbeAnswer::Refused`, `mm::LoginVerdict::probeRefused`,
  `mm::kProbeAnsweredRefused` and `Context::probeRefused` in the tree with no code
  able to produce any of them, so all four are gone and `probeAnsweredAs` is the
  role's `permissionName`. This is the one part of the change a consumer of
  `lib/agent-core/` can notice, and `CHANGELOG.md` says so. The **wire** did not
  move: `writeLoginSilentResult` still takes a string and
  `LoginSilentResult.ItIsTheOneReasonAndTheTierTheProbeWasAnsweredAt` still writes
  `"refused"` through it, because meshmaster.dk's `LoginSilentResult` documents
  that value for an agent whose companion emits `PUSH_CODE_LOGIN_FAIL` and this
  firmware is not the only agent.
- **`flood_.answered()` now fires only for a login reply.** It used to fire for
  anything that ended the wait. A flooded login that a *previous* request's reply
  wandered into was never answered, so the unscoped-retry counts
  (`agent/FloodScope.h`) are now about what the flood actually got. No test moved.
- **Two existing tests changed their outcome**, which the criteria asked for and
  which is worth naming twice: `ARefusedLoginIsUnauthorized` asserted
  `unauthorized` at **one** login with the CLI line never sent, and is now
  `AResponseThatIsNotALoginReplyIsDroppedAndTheSecondTryRunsTheJob` — `ok`, two
  logins, the line sent once. `AnAclLoginTheRepeaterRefusedIsFailedAtTheLogin`
  asserted `unauthorized`, and is now
  `AnAclLoginAnsweredWithSomethingElseIsFailedAtTheLoginAsASilenceIs` —
  `unreachable`, still `failedAt: login`, because the field says where a job
  failed and not why. No other existing assertion in the tree changed.
- **The spec and the task agreed otherwise.** EXIT-NODES §7 (the login, *What a
  wrong password actually looks like*, *One silence is reported as a refusal all
  the same*), §4.3 (*ACL entry gone*, *Our own logins*) and meshmaster.dk's
  `tasks/phase-4.32/186-a-refusal-with-nothing-in-it.md` say exactly what this
  implements, and 186's *Not in this task* names this task for the agent's half.
  Nothing in either repository asks a firmware to read a non-login response as a
  refusal; §7's *`unauthorized` from a refusal remains in the protocol* is about
  the protocol and the legacy reply, which is still parsed.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **One function for the whole wait, used by both logins.** `awaitLoginReply` is
  where the rule lives, rather than a check repeated in `loginOnce` and
  `probeReachable`: two copies of this rule is one copy that can rot, and the
  probe's is the one whose mistake costs a credential rotation its fallback. It is
  also what let the probe's own text test keep its shape — a host cannot run
  `JobRunner`, so that file's text is how the probe is held.
- **Eight drops per wait, and a wait that the drops do not extend.** A reply the
  deadline has passed is not awaited again, and a mesh that somehow handed the
  same reply back for ever cannot hold the loop: both are bounds on a board that
  has one thread and a watchdog. Eight is far more than a job can have in flight —
  one, by the budget of EXIT-NODES §8 — so the bound cannot be reached by anything
  the air does honestly.
- **A ring of eight tags, most recent kept, requests only.** The tags that matter
  are the recent ones; a `config.read`'s thirty-odd CLI lines are deliberately not
  in it, because a CLI reply carries no tag and their timestamps could only ever
  match a response's four bytes by accident. The ring is `Context`'s, so it dies
  with the job, as `IMesh::endJob` releases the peer.
- **`AgentMesh` filters nothing extra.** It could remember the job's tags itself
  and drop them before the library sees them, and that would be a second copy of
  the rule in the one mesh that needs it least. What it got instead is two
  comments saying that a tag-0 wait hands everything through on purpose and where
  the rule is.
- **One serial line per drop.** `jobs: job NN: login (acl) try 1: a late reply to
  one of this job's own requests was dropped` — the bench walk is *watch for it*,
  and a repeater that does this often has to be visible on the console without the
  job's page. No bytes of the dropped reply are printed, as no reply's bytes ever
  are.
- **The `FakeMesh` answer was renamed and re-cited, not deleted.**
  `login-refused` is now `login-another-reply`, and its citation moved from
  `fixture login-failed.hex` — a *companion frame*, which is not what the fake
  arms — to `examples/companion_radio/MyMesh.cpp:716-721 MyMesh::onContactResponse`,
  the function that turns such a response into `PUSH_CODE_LOGIN_FAIL`. That is an
  honest citation for bytes the agent must **not** read as a refusal, and
  `Citations.EveryAnswerOfTheFakeCitesAFixtureOrAFirmwareFunction` checks the
  function still exists at or above that line in the pinned source.
- **The evidence that each case fails without the fix was measured, not argued.**
  `git checkout master -- lib src` with every test file kept, one `pio test -e
  native -f test_runner`: eight failures, and exactly the eight cases this task
  wrote or rewrote. It is in the checklist because *each case fails without the
  fix* is a deliverable and not a claim to take on trust.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session can build, run the host tests and read the MeshCore source it builds
against. It cannot flash a board or hear a radio, so this list is Thomas's with
the board on the bench. It is `docs/console.md`'s walk item **22**, and the thing
that makes it unusual is that **it cannot be staged**: a late reply is a slow
repeater, and no console command produces one.

1. **Watch for the sequence.** On a repeater with an `active` `acl` pair through
   this exit node, far enough away or busy enough that a reply sometimes misses
   its wait, press *Read the neighbours* twice inside a session's lifetime so the
   second job finds the session live. What the job's log should read when it
   happens is in walk item 22; what must **never** appear again is `login (acl)
   try 1: refused`, and with it an `unauthorized` carrying no data. The serial
   console says it in one line too (`a late reply to one of this job's own
   requests was dropped`), so a repeater that does this often is visible without
   the job's page. AAR_LH through Risskov, and the four repeaters of the ten
   parked jobs, are where it has actually happened.
2. **The association stays put.** The point of the whole task: such a job must end
   `ok` with the reading, and the pair must still be `active` with no history line
   about a missing ACL entry and no `association_broken` alert. meshmaster.dk's
   p4.32-186 protects a pair from the *old* behaviour without flashing, so after
   that is deployed the two halves agree — this is the one to watch on the first
   day both are live.
3. **A repeater with no neighbours.** The empty page is the reply that would have
   read as *logged in as guest*; if one can be arranged (a repeater on a bench
   that has heard nobody), a *Read the neighbours* of it after a request that
   timed out must still say `logged in as read-only` and carry **no** tier on the
   result. This is the case the tag rule exists for and the only one a parse could
   never have caught.
4. **A rotation on a test repeater** (meshmaster.dk's `docs/ops/credentials.md` §6, walk item 17):
   the probe path still behaves — a silent first try, `reachability probe (blank
   login): answered at read-only`, and a silent second try is still `unauthorized`
   with `{"reason":"silentAfterReachable","probeAnsweredAs":"read-only"}`. What
   can no longer happen is `probeAnsweredAs: refused`; nothing on the server
   depended on it (meshmaster.dk prints the string and nothing else), but a
   rotation walked once after this is cheap.
5. **A discover whose direct login is slow.** The association wizard on a far
   repeater: the direct login along the server's path being unanswered must lead
   to `nothing came back along it: the path is cleared, so the next login floods`
   and then the flooded login, and the result's `loggedInAs` must be the tier the
   second login granted. Before this, a stray response made the discover report a
   path with no tier and the grant that followed was queued on a login nobody had
   answered.
6. **The Walter.** It has its own `IMesh` and takes this at its next `agent-core`
   sync; nothing of its link needs to change, which is the third deliverable. One
   look after that sync that a local job — answered at admin by the door, with no
   wait to be late for — still logs in in one line and carries no new log lines at
   all.
