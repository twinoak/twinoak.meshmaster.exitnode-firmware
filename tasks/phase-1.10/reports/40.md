# p1.10-40 — A local job is not on the air

The airtime budget exists to share **one radio channel** (EXIT-NODES §8). A job
that never touches the channel now neither waits for it nor spends it: `IMesh`
gained one defaulted virtual, `answersLocally()`, and `JobRunner::spend` returns
before it touches the gate when the mesh answers true.

**This board loses nothing and changes nothing.** `AgentMesh` keeps the
interface's default — the Heltec's radio is its own and it has no local door —
so every existing test, every pacing figure and both images are what they were
plus the method's bytes. The task is for the **first consumer**: on 7 November
2026 a 34-line `config.read` of a repeater-v2 unit's *own* repeater, served over
the UART through `LinkMesh`'s local door and so never on the air at all, was
paced at the server's *2 a minute, gap 20 s* anyway. `spend` → `idle` waited
about 40 s before the third line, which outlived the Walter's 30 s task watchdog
and reset it mid-job (job 22032); without the reset the read would have taken
eleven minutes for air it was not going to use, and a `telemetry` of the same
repeater waited 20 s for nothing. That is fix 2 of
`twinoak.meshcore.walter-firmware`'s `tasks/phase-8/FOLLOWUPS.md` — *A busy wait
below one tick still starves the watchdog* — the one its pile 9 could not make
itself, and it is the item *A local job is paced like the air* of the same file.

Two files of code, two of words, two of tests.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **A local job reports `transactions: 0`, and the budget is skipped rather
  than priced at nothing.** This is deliverable 2's question, and all three
  sources answer it the same way. EXIT-NODES §7 defines a transaction as *one
  request/response exchange with one repeater **over the mesh***; the .NET
  agent's own `JobContext` says *`Transactions` counts every **over-the-air**
  send* (`src/MeshMaster.Agent/Handlers.cs:25` at `fc7429b`); and §8's planned
  load is *what the schedules that route primarily through it will cost **on the
  air**, in transactions a minute, against the budget its owner set*
  (`PlannedLoad`). The figure is only worth anything because it is comparable
  with the budget it is weighed against, so an exchange that used no channel must
  not be in it. The **cost** of the choice is that the server's planned load
  over-estimates a unit that serves its own repeater locally — it prices every
  scheduled probe as air, because nothing tells it which repeater is behind which
  exit node's UART — and an over-estimate against a budget is the right way round,
  the same direction §6's lease arithmetic already errs in on purpose (*a lease
  may safely be too long and must never be too short*). Nothing in this firmware
  reads `ctx.transactions` for a decision; it is reported, remembered for
  `agent jobs`, and printed.
- **The gate is skipped, not told.** The alternative was a `took` that the gate
  ignores, or a second budget. Returning before `gate_.waitMs` keeps
  `gate().transmissions()` meaning *what went on the air* — which matters beyond
  the result, because `AgentLoop::transactionsLeft()` reads that same counter for
  the wake's `maxTransactionsThisWake` cap, so a local job does not eat a sleeping
  unit's wake allowance either.
- **The interface is asked at every `spend`, not once per job.** The contract is
  *true for the whole of the job*, so a cached copy in `Context` would say the
  same thing; asking each time is one line instead of three and cannot go stale.
  The method is documented `const`, cheap and non-blocking for exactly this
  reason, as `supports()` and `floodScope()` are.
- **One log line for the whole job, beside the `path …` line.** *this mesh
  answers for this repeater without the air: no airtime budget, no transaction* —
  said once, after `beginJob`, because a result that says *ok after 0
  transaction(s)* needs to say why on the same page. A line per exchange would
  have been thirty-four of them in a kilobyte.
- **The 34-line read's own local-door line is asserted on a short job.** A log of
  thirty-four entries does not fit the kilobyte buffer and task p1.10-38 drops
  whole entries from the front when it fills, so the line is genuinely gone from
  that result. The test says so in a comment and the third test asserts the line
  on a local `status`, whose log is short. Not a defect of either change: the
  buffer is bounded on purpose and the dropped entries are the oldest.
- **The fake answers the same bytes through the door as off it.** `FakeMesh` is a
  repeater and not a radio, so `setAnswersLocally(true)` changes the pacing and
  nothing else — which is the whole of the library's side of the door. It carries
  no `// answer:` marker and needs no citation, like `setFloodPathHashSize`
  beside it.
- **`docs/library.md`'s new row says "unreleased" where its neighbours say
  "since `0.5.0`".** The release task is what gives a method its version, and this
  task is explicitly not a release, so the row points at `CHANGELOG.md` for the
  version — the same choice task 39 made for `floodPathHashSize`.

**One thing deliberately left for a follow-up, not done here.** The wake still
*prices* a local job as air. `AgentLoop::wakeJobs` asks `transactionsOf(job)` and
`estimateJobMs(job)` — `n × (minGapSeconds + loginMs)` — before it runs a claimed
job, and reports it `failed`, *wake budget: about N s left of this wake and
config.read needs about M s*, at no transaction when the wake's slice is smaller
(`lib/agent-core/src/AgentLoop.cpp:697`). A 34-transaction local read still looks
like about seventeen minutes to that arithmetic, so a sleeping unit with a short
wake will refuse the very job this task just made fast. That is a different gate
from `spend`'s, it is the *consumer's* wake bound rather than the air's, the
Walter's pile 9 task 05 owns that end, and the task's deliverables name `spend`
and nothing else — so it is named here for the reviewer to open rather than
widened into this branch.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot flash a board or hear a radio — and this time it cannot do
something more: **nothing of this change is visible on this board at all.** The
Heltec has no local door, `AgentMesh` keeps the default `false`, and every path
through `spend` on this firmware is the path it was. The bench list is therefore
in two halves: what proves the Heltec is unchanged, and what proves the change
works, which needs a Walter.

**On the exit node (that nothing moved):**

1. **A `config.read` still paces exactly as it did.** Queue the daily
   `config.read` of a repeater through the exit node and read the job's page:
   *leased for 22 min 40 s, 34 transactions*, the transaction count the server
   priced it at, and the log's *waiting 20 s for the airtime budget* lines where
   they always were. A read that suddenly finishes in seconds, or one that reports
   0 transactions, would mean this board picked up a local door it has not got.
2. **The job log carries no new line.** The line *this mesh answers for this
   repeater without the air* must appear on **no** job of this board. If it does,
   `AgentMesh` is answering true, which it has no code to do.
3. **`agent jobs` and the heartbeat's `transactionsSpent` are unchanged.** The
   wake report's figure comes from the same gate counter; one wake of the usual
   jobs should spend what it spent before this firmware.

**On a repeater-v2 unit's Walter (that the change works), once its pile 9 syncs
this library and implements the virtual — this is its task 05's bench, listed
here because it is what this task is for:**

4. **The read that reset it.** Job 22032's shape — a `config.read` of the unit's
   **own** repeater, thirty keys — queued again. It should come back `ok` in
   **seconds**, with `transactions` **0**, *this mesh answers for this repeater
   without the air* as an early line of its log, no *waiting … s for the airtime
   budget* anywhere in it, and **no watchdog reset and no core dump**. `agent
   jobs` should show it; `LinkMesh::linkCounters()`' `local_jobs` and
   `local_requests` should have moved and `client_tx` should not have.
5. **A telemetry of the same repeater costs nothing and waits for nothing.** It
   waited 20 s for the gap on 7 November. It should now be immediate, at 0
   transactions.
6. **A job for a repeater that is *not* the unit's own still paces.** The same
   unit, a neighbouring repeater over the air: the gap and the per-minute cap must
   hold exactly as before, and the result must carry its real transaction count.
   This is the one that catches a door left open — a `setOwnKey` that matches too
   much, or an `answersLocally()` that stays true after the `endJob`.
7. **A local job after an over-the-air one does not shorten the next.** Run an
   over-the-air job, then a local one, then an over-the-air one. The third must
   still wait out the budget from the first. The host test asserts this on the
   fake clock; the bench is where the real gate and the real clock agree with it.
8. **The job's page reads sensibly at 0 transactions.** meshmaster.dk prints
   *ok after 0 transaction(s)* and its planned-load page still prices that
   repeater's schedule as air. Worth a look by eye, and worth a follow-up **in
   that repository** if the page reads as a job that did nothing rather than a job
   that used no channel.
9. **The wake does not refuse it.** The follow-up named above: if the unit's wake
   slice is shorter than the seventeen minutes `estimateJobMs` prices a
   34-transaction read at, the job comes back `failed`, *wake budget*, at no
   transaction — fast, and still not done. If the bench sees that, it is the
   follow-up and not this change.
