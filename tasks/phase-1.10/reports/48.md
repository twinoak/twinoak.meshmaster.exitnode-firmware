# p1.10-48 — A line that cannot be asked twice

`setperm <our own key> <level>` runs on a session whose key is, at that moment,
admin — and on stock firmware the session **is** the ACL entry. The moment the
line has run, the key is at `<level>`, and `onPeerDataRecv` runs a console line
only for a sender that `isAdmin()`: every further line from this key is dropped
without a word. So the retry of EXIT-NODES §5 cannot be answered, and a lost
"OK" ended the job `unreachable` with the line having done its work.
meshmaster.dk, 12 November 2026: 20 of 52 failed grants were exactly that, and
the restore after an admin job the same — association 3 found *elevated* seven
times in 39 days.

The line is now **sent once**, in `runAclSet` for our own key and in
`restoreAcl` alike, and what it did is read back with a **blank login** whose
reply carries the entry's own permission byte: answered at the level asked for
is *confirmed, by the login and not by an "OK"*; answered at admin means the
line never arrived, so it goes once more and is checked once more (two rounds at
most); a level-0 line answered at guest is the confirmation that the key is off;
and a silence confirms nothing and says so. A confirmed grant of our own key
**keeps no session**, so the verify that follows logs in by key.

## Deliverables, one by one

1. **The line is sent once, and its silence is checked with a blank login.**
   `JobRunner::writeOwnEntry(job, ctx, command, level, what)` is the whole of
   that rule, and the two places this board sends the line both go through it:
   `runAclSet` on our own key and `restoreAcl`. `cliLine` is called with
   `retry = false`, and on a silence `readOwnEntryLevel` sends one blank login —
   one transaction through the gate, this board's own key, no secret — and reads
   the role out of the reply. Four readings, in this order: the level asked for
   (confirmed, `byLogin`), admin (the line did not arrive: round two, and at most
   two), anything else (not confirmed), nothing (not confirmed, and nothing
   known). The log says which, in one sentence each, and the serial console says
   the check's answer in one line.
2. **Level 0.** `asked` is the level's own role, so a level-0 line is confirmed
   by a check answered at **guest** and by nothing else — which is what a
   repeater answers a key its access list does not hold, because
   `applyPermissions` **deletes** a guest-role entry rather than holding one. A
   silence is not a confirmation and the result says the line went out: a
   repeater with a guest password answers an unknown key with silence, and so
   does one that is off the air.
3. **A grant drops the session.** After the line has gone out on our own key the
   session is invalidated — confirmed or not — and the log says *the session is
   not kept, so the next job for this repeater logs in by key* (or *gone with the
   entry* at level 0). The one exception is a line the repeater **answered** with
   an error (`Err - bad pubkey`): it ran and did nothing, so the entry and the
   session are where they were and the session is kept, as before. The restore
   keeps its session at the level it put back, as it always did.
4. **The price.** `transactionsFor("acl.set")` and `kTransactionsForAclRestore`
   are both `kTransactionsForOwnEntryWrite` = the line and its check, and
   `transactionsOf` (which is also the lease and wake estimate,
   `AgentLoop::estimateJobMs`) therefore prices any job that may send that line
   one transaction higher than before. The second round is not in it, for the
   reason the deviations give and with the measurement behind it.
5. **The words.** `docs/library.md`'s new **§3.7** (*The one line that cannot be
   asked twice*), with the table of the four readings, the two consequences and
   the row of §3.4 that used to promise a retry; `docs/console.md`'s job-log
   section (the sequence as the page prints it) and bench walk items **23** and
   **24**, which are the *Not verified here* list below; `CHANGELOG.md`'s
   *Unreleased*, because a consumer that prices a wake sees the constant move.

## What the pinned source says about level 0 — the task's question

The task asks that the second reason for level 0 be checked in the pinned source
before anything is built on it. At `81dbae59`, on disk under
`.pio/libdeps/heltec_v3/MeshCore`:

- `ClientACL::applyPermissions` (`src/helpers/ClientACL.cpp:121-144`): a `perms`
  whose role is `PERM_ACL_GUEST` — which level 0 is — takes the `getClient`
  branch, `num_clients--`, and then `while (i < num_clients) clients[i] =
  clients[i + 1]`. The entry is **deleted and the later ones moved down**; it is
  not an entry held at guest. (And a second level-0 line for the same key finds
  no entry and returns false, which `handleCommand` answers `Err - invalid
  params` — one more reason not to retry it.)
- `MyMesh::onPeerDataRecv` (`examples/simple_repeater/MyMesh.cpp:663-758`):
  `client` is taken **before** the line runs (`ClientInfo* client =
  acl.getClientByIdx(i)`, line 670) and the reply is built with `client->id` and
  sent along `client->out_path` (line 751). Nothing re-reads the slot after
  `handleCommand`.

So the task's reading is right, with one refinement worth having: the reply is
lost **unless our entry was the last live slot**. If the deleted slot is *after*
ours nothing moves under us; if it is at or below ours, `clients[ours]` now holds
the next client — except when ours was the last, where the shift loop stops
before it and the slot still holds us. In one line: the "OK" comes home when
`deleted > ours || ours == count - 1`. `test/support/FakeMesh.h` is that reading
as code (`answer: cli-setperm-zero`, cited to both functions), which is why
`ARevokeIsConfirmedByABlankLoginAnsweredAtGuest` needs no fault knob at all and
why bench walk **24** asks for the case with our key first and the case with our
key last. **Nobody has tried it on a board**, and that walk is how we find out if
the reading is wrong.

One consequence beyond this task's scope, said here for the reviewer: the same
shift misaddresses the "OK" of a level-0 line for **somebody else's** key whose
slot is at or below ours, and the retry of that line (which this task leaves in
place, because the session survives it) is answered `Err - invalid params` by a
repeater that already ran it — so such a job would report `failed` with the entry
gone. No job meshmaster.dk sends is one: an association's grant and its revoke
are always this exit node's own key (EXIT-NODES §4.1 step 3), which is why
nothing was changed for it.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The check is priced once, not twice: `kTransactionsForOwnEntryWrite` is 2 and
  not 4.** Deliverable 4 asks that `transactionsFor` and the lease estimate
  *count the check where it can happen*, with *an estimate may be too high and
  never too low*. Priced as a strict ceiling — two lines and two checks, four —
  it broke two cases of `test_loop`
  (`AJobTheWakesTransactionCapHasNoRoomForIsFailedTheSameWay` and
  `TheAirtimeGateStillPacesAWakeWithRoomForSixTransactions`), and they were right
  to break: the restore is counted **wherever there is a grant**, so a ceiling of
  four prices an ordinary `acl`-class status read through an associated pair at
  **six** transactions and a one-wake unit with a slice of four or six
  transactions would answer the daily probes `failed`, *wake budget*. That is a
  worse failure than the one being priced for. So the estimate is the line and
  the one check it needs, and **the second round is beyond it — exactly as the
  login's own second try has always been**: `kTransactionsForLogin` is 1 and
  `loginSequence` sends two tries (three on the rotation path). What bounds both
  is the lease and the gate, which refuse a send rather than a job. The constant,
  `Airtime.h`'s comment, the `test_airtime` case, `docs/library.md` §3.7 and the
  changelog all say this in those words, and the two `test_loop` comments were
  re-written rather than their numbers raised.
- **`tools\verify.ps1` does not exist in this repository.** The last acceptance
  criterion names it (so do tasks 49 and 50, and 47 reported the same), and there
  is no such script in `tools/`. What it stands for is run instead, and by hand:
  the native suite in a container, `pio run -e heltec_v3`, and the four other
  steps of CI's native job. The rails themselves — verified TLS, no
  `setInsecure`, no secret in a log line — are native tests (`test_tls_policy`,
  `test_seams`, `test_credentials`) and are in the 601. Either the script should
  be written or the two remaining task files should say what this one says; that
  is the reviewer's call and not this task's scope.
- **`AclSetResult` is now absent when the line never went out**, for our own key.
  meshmaster.dk's p4.32-188 states as a fact that *`AclSetResult` is there with
  `confirmed: false` exactly when the line went out, and absent when the job
  never got that far*, and that is what its branch between *the verify decides*
  and *failed at `granting`* is keyed on. It was not quite true: a lease that ran
  out, or a packet that could not be built, after the login but before the line
  wrote the data all the same. For our own key it is true now (the line is sent
  once, so `Timeout` and `Failed` from the exchange mean nothing left the board),
  and the log says *the line never went out, so this exit node's own entry is
  untouched*. The foreign-key branch is unchanged, retry and all.
- **The outcome of a line that went out and could not be confirmed is
  `unreachable`, not `failed`.** That is what the exchange came to and what 188
  reads (*A grant ends `unreachable` with `{"confirmed": false, …}`: the flow is
  at `verifying`*), and it is deliberate even where the check was answered
  *below* the level asked for and the line demonstrably did not take: the verify
  decides, at no risk, rather than this firmware ruling out a grant on one
  reading. A line the repeater **answered** with an error is `failed`, as before.
- **The server's table and this firmware's row now differ by one.**
  EXIT-NODES §7 prices the restore at `TransactionCosts.AclRestore` = 1 and
  `acl.set` at 1, and meshmaster.dk's planned load reads those. A repeater that
  answers the line still spends 1; one that does not spends 2. The spec is in the
  other repository: the reviewer may want §7's two rows to say *1, or 2 when the
  line is not answered*, and `PlannedLoad`'s number is a planning average rather
  than a ceiling, so it may be right to leave it at 1. Nothing here depends on
  the answer.
- **A `setperm <own key> 3` would be confirmed by a check answered at admin**,
  because the level asked for is tested before the *did it arrive* reading. No
  caller sends one — `restoreAcl` gates on `aclLevel < kPermAdmin`, and an
  association grants read-only or read-write — but the order is deliberate and
  the code says so.
- **The spec and the task agreed otherwise.** EXIT-NODES §4.1 steps 3 and 4 (the
  cost: *one ACL transaction with a login of its own* — which is what deliverable
  3 restores), §4.3 (*Our own logins*, *Revoke*) and §7 (*Every admin-password
  session ends with the restore*, *An `acl.set` on our own key sends no second
  `setperm`: the command is the level*) say exactly what this implements.
  meshmaster.dk's `tasks/phase-4.32/188` and `189` are the server's half and
  neither asks anything else of the firmware; 189's own remedy — *a blank login
  that is answered admin is put back by the job that made it* — is the rule this
  task leaves working and now also checks.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **One function for both callers.** `writeOwnEntry` holds the rule and
  `readOwnEntryLevel` holds the check, rather than the sequence being written
  twice: the grant's copy and the restore's copy would be one copy that can rot,
  and they are the same line with the same fault. The session bookkeeping is the
  **caller's**, because that is where the two differ (a grant keeps none, a
  restore keeps its own).
- **The check stores no session**, as the rotation probe stores none. The reply
  is evidence about an entry, not a session to spend: `restoreAcl` puts its own
  session back at the level it restored, and `runAclSet` drops the repeater's.
- **The check does not move `ctx.stage`.** It is a login, but it is not *the
  job's* login: a job that ends after it still reports `failedAt: request`, which
  is where the job failed. Naming the login would tell the owner's page that a
  stored password is suspect because a `setperm` went unanswered.
- **The session is kept when the repeater answered the line and refused it.** The
  entry did not move, so the admin session is still good and the next job need
  not log in again. Everything else — confirmed, or unknown — drops it.
- **One `what` for both rounds**, so the job's log shows two `try 1` lines for the
  same line with the sentence *sending it once more* between them, and
  `timesSent` is 2. A second label would have read as a different command.
- **Eight drops still bound the check's own wait**, because it is
  `awaitLoginReply` (task p1.10-47) and nothing else: the reply this check must
  drop is the very "OK" whose loss it is checking, and reading a text frame as a
  login reply would have made its bytes into the entry's level. This is the
  dependency the task names, and it is why 47 had to land first.
- **Two fault knobs in the fake, line-keyed and counted.**
  `loseTheNextLines(line, n)` is a line that never reaches the repeater and
  `loseTheNextLineReplies(line, n)` is a line that **ran** and whose reply never
  came home — which is the field's fault and which neither `silenceLine` (never
  ran) nor `replySlowly` (counts every reply, a login's included) could say. They
  are keyed by line because the restore's line has to be lost inside a
  thirty-odd-line `config.read` without touching the `get`s, and counted because
  the two rounds send the same text.
- **The fake deletes the entry at level 0 and misaddresses the reply.** It is a
  second reading of the firmware (`answer: cli-setperm-zero`, cited to
  `applyPermissions` and `onPeerDataRecv`), it makes the fake's blank login behave
  as the real one does afterwards — through the password compare, so a guest
  password matters — and it is what lets the level-0 tests be written without a
  fault knob at all. It also fixed a quieter divergence: with the entry merely
  zeroed, the fake answered a *request* from a key the firmware would no longer
  know at all.
- **Each case was measured without the fix, not argued.** `git stash push -- lib
  src` with every test file kept, then `pio test -e native -f test_runner -f
  test_airtime -f test_config_read`: **9 failed, 105 succeeded** — the seven new
  cases, the rewritten `AnAclSetOnOurOwnKeyIsSentOnceAndKeepsNoSession` and
  `test_config_read`'s price — and `test_airtime` does not **compile** at all,
  because `mm::kTransactionsForOwnEntryWrite` is this task's. Nothing else in
  those three suites moved. With the fix: 137 of 137, and 601 of 601 over the
  whole suite.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session can build, run the host tests and read the MeshCore source it builds
against. It cannot flash a board or hear a radio, so this list is Thomas's with
the board on the bench. It is `docs/console.md`'s walk items **23** and **24**,
and the second of them is the one that matters most: it is a reading of the
firmware that nobody has tried.

1. **A grant whose "OK" is lost** (walk item 23). The ordinary grant first, to
   see nothing changed: `cli 'setperm <key>... 1' try 1: ok`, *the repeater
   answered 'OK'*, 2 transactions. Then one whose reply misses its 16 s wait — a
   repeater at the far end of the garden, or one busy answering something else.
   The log must read *no answer, and this line cannot be asked twice*, *the entry
   check (blank login): answered at read-only*, *confirmed by the login and not
   by an "OK"*; `jobs` counts **3**, the job is **ok**, and `get acl` on the
   repeater's own console lists this board's key at `01`. What must never happen
   again is a grant that ends `unreachable` with the entry set.
2. **The verify after it logs in.** The job after a confirmed grant must show a
   login of its own (`login (acl) try 1: logged in as read-only`) and never
   `session reused` — which is what makes the verify a proof of the entry rather
   than of the admin password. meshmaster.dk's job 26077 is the one that did not.
3. **A revoke of a key that is not last in the table** (walk item 24, and the
   reading above). Grant this exit node's key **first** and another key after it,
   then *Revoke* from meshmaster.dk: the "OK" should be addressed to the other
   key and never arrive, the check should be answered **guest**, and the job
   should be `ok` with `confirmed: true` at 3 transactions. Then the same with
   ours **last**: the "OK" should come home at 2. If the first case answers its
   line after all, the reading of `applyPermissions` and `onPeerDataRecv` in
   `test/support/FakeMesh.h` is wrong and the fake needs correcting — this walk
   is the only way to find out.
4. **A revoke on a repeater with a guest password.** The third case of the same
   item: the blank login is silence, so the job is `unreachable` with
   `confirmed: false`, and meshmaster.dk (after p4.32-188) must say *the command
   went out and no answer came back, which is usual for a key's own removal*
   rather than *the ACL entry may still be on the device*.
5. **A restore whose "OK" is lost**, which is the same line at the other end of
   an admin session: a *Read the configuration* of a far repeater, often enough
   that one restore goes unanswered. Its log must read *as granted, which the
   blank login confirmed and no "OK" did*, the result's `aclRestored` must be
   `true`, `jobs` must count **22** rather than 21, and the next day's read must
   **not** list this board's key at `03`. The association's history must stop
   saying *elevated*; after meshmaster.dk's p4.32-189 that sentence is a read at
   `acl` and not an `acl.set` at admin, so the two halves want watching together
   on the first day both are live.
6. **The two rounds.** A grant whose *line* is lost rather than its reply — hard
   to stage deliberately; a repeater rebooting in the second between the login
   and the line is the realistic way. The log must read *the line did not arrive,
   and the session is admin again - sending it once more*, and `jobs` must count
   4. Two losses in a row must leave `jobs` at 5, `unreachable`, and `sessions`
   with none for that repeater.
7. **The Walter.** It has its own `IMesh` and takes this at its next
   `agent-core` sync. Two things to look at there: a local job answered at admin
   by the door needs no check at all (its lines are answered inside the link, so
   `writeOwnEntry` returns at the first reply), and a unit's own repeater is
   handed no grant, so the restore never runs for it. The one thing its pile must
   notice is the price: a job through a pair with a grant is one transaction
   dearer in `transactionsOf`, so a wake slice sized exactly to the old number
   will refuse one job it used to run.
