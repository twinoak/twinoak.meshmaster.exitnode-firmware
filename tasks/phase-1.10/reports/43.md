# p1.10-43 — A local job is priced as local

Task 40 made a job the mesh answers **without the air** skip the airtime gate
while it runs. The wake prices a claimed job **before** it runs, and priced it as
air: `n × (minGapSeconds + loginMs)`. A 34-line `config.read` of a unit's own
repeater — the read that reset a Walter on 7 November 2026 — came out at
34 × (20 s + 8 s) = **952 s** of gaps for a channel it was never going to touch,
against the Walter's 1 200 s exchange bound: 248 s of margin, and a shorter
bound, a longer key list or a larger `minGapSeconds` from the server would have
refused, in seconds and at no transaction, the very job 40 had just made fast.
That is the Walter's pile-9 follow-up *The wake still prices a local job as air*,
and the thing report p1.10-40 named for the reviewer rather than widen into its
own branch.

It is priced as local now: **272 s**, its thirty-four reply waits, and **0
transactions**. The wake asks one question to find out — `IMesh` gained a second
defaulted virtual, `answersLocallyFor(publicKeyHex)`, which is 40's door asked
for a *key* instead of for the job in progress — and `wakeJobs` reads that one
answer into both of its bounds.

**This board loses nothing and changes nothing.** `AgentMesh` keeps the
interface's default, the Heltec has no local door, and every path through the
wake's pricing on this firmware is the path it was, to the second and to the
word. Five files of code, two of words, four of tests, and **40 bytes** of
flash.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The shape is a predicate on a key, `answersLocallyFor(publicKeyHex)`** — the
  task asked for the shape to be chosen and justified. Three were possible. A
  predicate on the **claim** (`answersLocallyFor(const ClaimedJob&)`) would have
  made `IMesh.h` include the job envelopes, which it deliberately includes
  nothing of — the file's own header says so, and the interface is meant to be
  implementable with nothing of this repository's job model in view. An accessor
  (`const char* localDoorKey()`, or `ownKey()`) would have moved the comparison
  into the library and assumed there is exactly **one** such repeater, which is
  true of today's Walter and is not the interface's business to assume. The
  predicate assumes neither: it is the question the library actually has, the key
  is the one thing both ends already agree on (64 lowercase hex, as the claim
  carries it and as `beginJob` takes it), and on the first consumer it is
  satisfied by the expression `beginJob` already computes —
  `own_key_known_ && sameKey(key, own_key_)` — so the two answers cannot drift
  apart there. Documented with the contract that they must agree, and with
  *a key you cannot parse is false, not an error*, because a wake that cannot
  price a malformed key must not fail differently from the `beginJob` that will
  refuse it a moment later.
- **Two virtuals, not one.** `answersLocally()` could have been retired in favour
  of asking `answersLocallyFor(job.repeaterKey)` everywhere, which would have made
  the predicate literally singular. It was not: the Walter's `LinkMesh` already
  overrides `answersLocally()` (its pile 9, task 05), removing it would break that
  tree the moment this library is synced, and the task's own deliverable 1 says
  *its contract must agree with 40's `answersLocally()`* — which presumes 40's is
  still there. The agreement is therefore a documented contract, a seam test on
  the shape, and a runner test that computes both from one piece of state.
- **A local job is priced at `transactionsOf(job) × replyMs`, not at some smaller
  figure.** The exchanges do not go away — a 34-line read is thirty-four lines
  through the door too — and each one still waits for a reply, which on the real
  door is a UART round trip and on the air is `job.loginMs`. The claim's own reply
  bound is the only number either end has for it, and an estimate a battery
  spends against **may be too high and must never be too low** (the rule
  `transactionsOf` is already written to, and §6's lease arithmetic before it). So
  only the gap falls away. A local 34-line read is 272 s where it really takes
  seconds, which is the right way round.
- **The transaction price is `0`, and the check is left to decide for itself.**
  `price > left` with `price` 0 is false for every `left`, including 0, so a wake
  whose air allowance is spent can still run a local job — which is the same
  answer `spend` gives it, and the reason 40 kept the gate's counter meaning *what
  went on the air*. Written as `local ? 0 : transactionsOf(job)` rather than as a
  branch around the check, so that one `price` is still what the refusal message
  prints.
- **The refusal says why it was priced the way it was.** *… and config.read needs
  about 272 s, **answered without the air***, appended only when the job is local.
  Without it, a bench reading *needs about 272 s* for a job it has just been told
  is thirty-four transactions at a 20 s gap has no way to tell a local price from
  a wrong one. The sentence lives in a `why[]` buffer that grew from 128 to 160
  bytes to hold it (`snprintf`, so it was never at risk of overrunning; the old
  size would have truncated the new clause).
- **`estimateJobMs` takes the answer rather than asking for it.** It is a `const`
  method with the mesh to hand and could have asked itself, which would have been
  two questions where the whole point is one. The argument has no default value on
  purpose: a future caller has to decide what the door says, and the seam test
  asserts that no `estimateJobMs(job)` exists.
- **The claim's own sizing was left alone, and is named here as the remaining
  bound.** `claimMax()` asks for no more jobs than `maxTransactionsThisWake` can
  hold at `kTransactionsPerJobAtLeast` (2) apiece, so a wake whose transaction
  allowance is spent asks the server for **no jobs at all** — local or not. That
  cannot be fixed with this predicate: it is asked before the claim, and nothing
  can know which repeaters a claim will bring back until it answers. It is not the
  bug this task is for (the Walter's cap is sized to the dearest job at every band
  but `critical`, and a local job now spends none of it once claimed), and
  narrowing it would mean teaching the *server* which repeater is behind which
  UART, which is meshmaster.dk's end and not a line of this repository. Named for
  the reviewer, in the test that holds the rest of it
  (`ALocalJobCostsTheWakesTransactionCapNothing`'s own comment) as well as here.
- **`FakeMesh::setAnswersLocally(bool)` kept its meaning.** The flat flag — *the
  door answers for whatever repeater a job names* — is what tasks 40 and 41 wrote
  their cases against, and it still short-circuits both predicates, so not one of
  them moved. The keyed door is a second, sharper setter beside it rather than a
  replacement.
- **`docs/library.md`'s new row says "unreleased" where its neighbours say "since
  `0.5.0`".** The release task is what gives a method its version and this task is
  explicitly not a release, so the row points at `CHANGELOG.md` — the same choice
  tasks 39 and 40 made.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot flash a board or hear a radio, and — as with task 40 — **nothing
of this change is visible on this board at all**: the Heltec has no local door,
`AgentMesh` keeps the default, and `loop()` does not price jobs in the first
place (only `wake()` does, and this board never calls it). The bench list is
again in two halves.

**On the exit node (that nothing moved):**

1. **A `config.read` still paces and still reports as it did.** Queue the daily
   `config.read` of a repeater through the exit node: *leased for 22 min 40 s*, 34
   transactions, the *waiting 20 s for the airtime budget* lines where they always
   were. This board's pricing is not exercised by `loop()` at all, so what this
   checks is that the 40 bytes changed nothing around it.
2. **No job of this board ever says *answered without the air*.** The clause is
   only appended to a *wake budget* refusal of a local job, and this board has
   neither. If it appears, `AgentMesh` is answering the new virtual true, which it
   has no code to do.
3. **`agent jobs` and the heartbeat's `transactionsSpent` are unchanged.** One
   wake of the usual jobs should spend what it spent before this firmware.

**On a repeater-v2 unit's Walter (that the change works), once its pile 10 task
01 syncs this library and implements the virtual — this is that task's bench,
listed here because it is what this task is for:**

4. **The read that would have been refused.** Job 22032's shape — a `config.read`
   of the unit's **own** repeater, thirty keys — queued on a unit whose exchange
   bound has been set *below* 952 s and above 272 s (`kBackendBoundMs` in
   `core/cycle/wake_cycle.h`; 400 s is the host test's figure and would do). Before
   this change that is `failed`, *wake budget: about N s left of this wake and
   config.read needs about 952 s*, at no transaction, in seconds. It must now come
   back **`ok`**, in seconds, at `transactions` **0**. That is the whole task, on
   the bench, and it is the one item that cannot be checked any other way.
5. **The refusal, when it is a real one, reads as local.** Set the bound below
   272 s and queue the same read: `failed`, *needs about 272 s, answered without
   the air*. The figure and the clause are what tell a reader the bound is the
   wake's and not the air's.
6. **A job for a repeater that is *not* the unit's own is priced as air.** The
   same unit, a neighbouring repeater, the same wake: the refusal must carry the
   **952 s** figure and no clause, and a wake large enough must pace the job at the
   gap and count its transactions. This is the one that catches a
   `answersLocallyFor` that matches too much — a `setOwnKey` not yet set, a hex
   comparison that ignores case wrongly, or a predicate that answers true for
   everything.
7. **The two answers agree on the unit.** The job the wake priced as local must be
   the job that runs off the air: `ok`, `transactions` 0, *this mesh answers for
   this repeater without the air* in its log, `LinkMesh::linkCounters()`'
   `local_jobs` moved and `client_tx` not. A job priced local that then pays the
   gate (or the reverse) means the Walter's two overrides are computed from
   different state, which is what the host test
   `TheDoorIsKeyedToARepeaterSoAJobForAnotherIsStillOnTheAir` holds for the fake
   and only a unit can hold for the real door.
8. **A wake with no transactions left in its cap still runs a claimed local job.**
   Reachable only when a claim brought back a local job and an earlier job of the
   same wake then spent the cap: the local one must still run, at 0 transactions,
   rather than come back *wake budget: 0 transaction(s) left*. The host test holds
   it on the fake clock.
9. **A wake whose cap is spent before the claim still asks for nothing.** The
   bound named under *Decisions* above: that is unchanged and correct, and the
   bench should expect *this wake has 0 transaction(s) left of its N, which is less
   than a job: no claim is made* exactly as before. Worth one look so that it is
   not read as this change failing.
