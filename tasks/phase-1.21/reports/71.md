# p1.21-71 — The way back is told

Before every login that goes out direct, the exit node now tells the repeater the way back to
itself: `createPathReturn` with the job's path reversed, sent direct along that path. Replies then
come home by the hops the requests went out by — the hops a trace measures — instead of being
flooded at every repeater that can hear them, and a job's log says how each reply came.

## What was built

**`IMesh::tellTheWayBack()`** (`lib/agent-core/include/agent/IMesh.h`), returning a `WayBack`:
`How::Told` / `Failed` / `NotTold`, the hops it named, and `clearMs`. **Its default tells nothing
and says so**, so the Walter's mesh and a host's compile and behave exactly as they did and the
library puts one sentence in the job's log about it. `MeshReply` gained `route`
(`mm::ReplyRoute`: `NotKnown`, `Direct`, `Flood`).

**On the board** (`AgentMesh::tellTheWayBack`): the job's path reversed at its own width,
`createPathReturn` to the peer, no extra payload, through `dispatch` — so it goes direct along the
out path and its frame is logged like any other. `AgentMesh` fills `MeshReply::route` from the
packet's own header at every reply it hands over (`routeOf`). `onPeerPathRecv` still returns
**false** and its comment now says why the reciprocal path is the runner's send and not MeshCore's.

**In the runner**: `tellTheWayBack` before every login that goes out direct (each try), then
`idle(clearMs)`, then the login; one more telling before the job's next send when a reply came
home flooded or a flood taught a path, **once a job**; one more before the first send of a job
that reuses a session whose claim names another path. `RepeaterSession` remembers the path it was
opened along (`SessionStore::put` gained three defaulted parameters). Every answered exchange's
log line now says how the reply came beside how the request went.

**The fake repeater** (`test/support/FakeMesh.h`) holds an `out_path` per key of its access list,
written by a way back from that key and by nothing else, dropped from a key it does not hold,
forgotten by a flooded login of that key — and answers direct along it when it holds one and
flooded when it does not. Knobs: `loseTheNextWaysBack(n)`, `theWayBackIsDead()`,
`setWayBackClearMs(ms)`, `tellsNoWayBack()`. Four citations were added to its manifest and
`Citations.EveryAnswerOfTheFakeCitesAFixtureOrAFirmwareFunction` walks them against the pinned
source on disk.

## The clearing time, and what the login's slot costs now

`AgentMesh::clearMsFor(bytes, hops)` is the packet's airtime once for this board's transmission
and, for each hop, the longest that hop may hold it plus its own transmission of it. **No number
is picked by hand:**

- the airtime is `Radio::getEstAirtimeFor(bytes)`, which on this board is RadioLib's
  `getTimeOnAir(len) / 1000` at the preset `platformio.ini` sets (`LORA_SF=8`, `LORA_BW=62.5`,
  `LORA_CR` defaulting to 5 in `CustomSX1262::std_init`, preamble 16, CRC on, no low-data-rate
  optimise — RadioLib's `SX126x::ldrOptimize` is 0);
- the hop's delay is MeshCore's own: `MyMesh::getDirectRetransmitDelay`
  (`examples/simple_repeater/MyMesh.cpp:551-554`) draws
  `getRNG()->nextInt(0, 5 * airtime * direct_tx_delay_factor + 1)`, and
  `direct_tx_delay_factor` is **0.3** out of the box (same file `:894`). Five times three tenths
  is **three halves** of the airtime, which is `kHopDelayNumerator / kHopDelayDenominator` in
  `AgentMesh.h`.

The frame is the header and the `path_len` byte (2), the path (`hops × size`), and the PATH
payload — a one-byte destination hash, a one-byte source hash, the two-byte MAC and the plaintext
(`path_len`, the hops, a dummy type byte and four random bytes) zero-padded to the cipher block,
so 20 bytes for every path that fits in ten bytes of hops:

| hops (two-byte hashes) | frame | airtime | clearing time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (a direct pair) | 22 B | 238 ms | **238 ms** |
| 1 | 24 B | 259 ms | **906 ms** |
| 2 | 26 B | 259 ms | **1 553 ms** |
| 4 | 30 B | 279 ms | **3 067 ms** |

(For scale, the worst path this firmware will carry — four hops of three-byte hashes — is a 50-byte
frame, 381 ms of airtime and 4 189 ms.)

**What the gate and the lease see.** A login's slot is **one** more packet and **one** transaction
still: `spend()` is called once, `gate_.took()` once, and `ctx.transactions` moves by one. The
airtime a login costs grows by that frame — 238 to 279 ms on top of the login's own, which at the
Danish preset is of the same order, so a login's airtime roughly doubles and a whole job's grows by
the fraction a login is of it (a `status` read: one login of three transactions). In time, the slot
is 0.2 to 3.1 s longer.

**No job is tighter in its lease**, and the arithmetic is why: `gate_.took()` is called at the
start of the slot, before the way back, so the sequence of transaction *starts* is unchanged — the
next slot still begins `minGapSeconds` after this one did, and the clearing wait is absorbed by the
gap rather than added to it. At the conservative default of EXIT-NODES §8 (two a minute, twenty
seconds apart) 3.1 s of clearing sits inside a 20 s gap with sixteen to spare, and
`spend()`'s own lease check is unmoved because `clock_->now()` has advanced by exactly what `wait`
has shrunk by. A job only ends later where the clearing time exceeds the budget's gap, which needs
a gap under four seconds and a four-hop path; a board whose server names such a budget has already
chosen to pay for the air. `RunnerTest.TheWayBackAndTheLoginAreOneSlotOfTheGateAndOneTransaction`
holds the gap at the default budget with a way back of 1 000 ms in it.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The reachability probe (the blank login of the rotation path) is not itself preceded by a way
  back; it only spends one that is owed.** The task says *before every login that goes out direct,
  each try of it*, and the two tries of a `LoginSequence` are `loginOnce`; the probe between them
  is a different thing in every line of this tree (*reachability probe (blank login)*). Telling one
  before all three would have put three identical packets on the air inside one transaction. The
  probe does spend an owed telling, so a flooded probe reply is still repaired.
- **A job the mesh answers without the air tells none** (`IMesh::answersLocally`): there are no
  hops to clear and no reply to route. `RunnerTest.ALocalJobTellsNoWayBack`.
- **The second telling is once a job and not once a reply**, whichever of the two reasons bought it
  (a flooded reply, or a path a flood taught). A way back told twice is the same way back and a
  repeater that ignored two will ignore three; what bounds a job's sends should be the job.
- **A session remembers the path its *claim* carried**, not the path the way back happened to be
  told from, which is what the task's words say (*another path in its claim*) and what the
  acceptance criterion asks of the two tests. The one case where the two differ — a job whose path
  was forgotten mid-job and replaced by one a flood taught — heals itself at the next job: the
  claim's path no longer matches what the repeater holds, the first reply comes home flooded, and
  the way back goes once more.
- **The reply's route is one short phrase and the way back is one short line**, because
  `JobLog` is 1 024 bytes and drops whole entries off the front when it fills — the hazard task
  p1.10-47's own comment names. The reason a second telling went out is **inside** the telling's
  line (*way back again (the reply came flooded)*) rather than a line of its own, for the same
  reason.
- **Five existing tests were edited**, and none of them for the default member:
  - `FloodTest.AnUnscopedBoardFloodsUnscopedAndCountsItAsSuch`,
    `FloodTest.AFloodedLoginSendsTryOneScopedAndTryTwoUnscoped` and
    `FloodTest.ADirectSendCarriesNoScopeAndCountsNothing` looked for `"(flood, unscoped)"` and
    `"(direct)"` **with the closing bracket**, which an answered exchange's line no longer ends
    with: it is `(direct, reply direct)` now. The send's own shape is unchanged and is still what
    they assert.
  - `Sessions.TheSlotsAreReadableForTheConsoleAndHoldNoSecret` holds
    `sizeof(mm::RepeaterSession)` against its fields; the session gained the path it was opened
    along, so those fields are in the sum now. A hop hash is the server's own and public by
    construction — it travels in every claim and in the header of every direct packet — so the
    rail the test is named for is untouched, and the test now also asserts that a session put with
    no path carries none.
  - `LoopTest.TheAirtimeGateStillPacesAWakeWithRoomForSixTransactions` measured the budget's gap
    between consecutive **sends**. A transaction now starts at its way back and its command
    follows inside the same slot, so the gap to measure is between transaction *starts*: the test
    reads `mesh.gapsMs()`, which is exactly that and is what every other gap test already used.
  - Two more had a **log line fall off** rather than an assertion go wrong, and are noted here
    because the evidence really is gone:
    `RunnerTest.AConsoleReplyThatCameTooLateIsNotStoredAsTheNextLinesValue` (a five-key
    `config.read` whose log was already at the kilobyte; its earliest two entries — the retry's
    *no reply* and the one sentence about the prefixes — are dropped now, and the entry the test is
    about, the dropped console reply, survives and is still asserted; the prefix sentence is held
    by `RunnerTest.EveryConsoleLineOfAJobCarriesItsOwnPrefixAndNoTwoAreTheSame` on a shorter job)
    and `RunnerTest.ARevokeIsConfirmedByABlankLoginAnsweredAtGuest` (whose **last** sentence,
    *the session is gone with the entry*, is cut by the kilobyte; what it says is asserted of the
    store by `RunnerTest.AfterAConfirmedGrantTheNextAclJobLogsIn`).
- **`MeshCounters::tx` counts the way back**, because `Dispatcher`'s `logTx` counts every packet
  that leaves the radio and this is one. So `status`'s `tx` is now *packets* and not *transactions*
  for a board with paths; the figure the server is priced on, the result's `transactions`, is
  unmoved. Nothing in the library reads `tx` (`docs/library.md` §3.2 says so) and no test holds it.
- **`direct_tx_delay_factor` is taken at its default of 0.3.** A repeater whose owner set
  `d_txdelay` higher holds a direct packet longer than the clearing time allows for, and then the
  login races the way back at the first hop: that reply comes home flooded and the way back is told
  once more, which is the same recovery as a packet lost on the air. Asking the repeater for the
  value would be a transaction, and the task's scope is one short packet.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot flash a board or hear a radio. What is left for the bench, with the board:

1. **A real repeater takes the packet.** The host's repeater is a fake written from MeshCore's
   source; nothing here proves `MyMesh::onPeerPathRecv` at `81dbae59` stores what this firmware
   sends. After the release: **a *Read now* of TwinOak Ebeltoft through Risskov**, and the job's
   log should read `way back: 2 hop(s), … ms to clear` and then `(direct, reply direct)` at the
   login and at both requests. If it reads `reply flooded` twice the packet is not landing;
   `log.frames on` prints the way back's frame as hex with its header, which is where to look (a
   `PAYLOAD_TYPE_PATH` of 0x0E, route DIRECT, and the two hops reversed in the payload).
2. **Ebeltoft's `sentDirect` moves.** It moved by five across seven hours of three requests an hour
   on 22 November. With the way back taken it should move by **three or more an hour**. Read from
   meshmaster.dk's counters a few hours after the release.
3. **The clearing time is enough on the air.** 1.5 s at two hops is this board's arithmetic over
   `getEstAirtimeFor` and the repeater's default `d_txdelay`; a repeater configured with a larger
   `d_txdelay`, or a hop that is busy, can still make the login overtake the way back. The symptom
   is a job whose log says `way back`, then `(direct, reply flooded)`, then
   `way back again (the reply came flooded)` and a direct reply after it — which is the recovery
   working, not a failure. Worth reading once on each pinned pair.
4. **A pair that is good out and poor back.** This is the one thing the task makes less forgiving:
   a reply has one way home now, and a link that is good out and poor back fails where a flood
   might have got through. The job ends `unreachable` exactly as a silence has always ended it. The
   trace shows both directions and a pin is the cure; `meshmaster.dk`'s task 256 is the flood a
   pair that is **not** pinned gets back.
5. **A direct neighbour's empty path.** `copyPath(…, 0)` reading as *direct* and not as
   `OUT_PATH_UNKNOWN` is read off the source; a repeater this exit node hears at zero hops would
   show it. None of the pinned pairs is one today.
6. **The airtime a board really spends.** The table above is `getEstAirtimeFor`'s estimate.
   `status`'s duty-cycle figures after a day of reads are the measurement.
