# p1.11-53 — The repeater's clock

A login reply from a repeater begins with four bytes of the repeater's own clock. This firmware
has read them at every login since task p1.1-08 — they are `MeshReply::tag`, and a login is
awaited with tag `0` for exactly that reason — and has thrown them away every time.
meshmaster.dk's task p4.37-210 gave them somewhere to go. This is the other half: **every job
that made a login of its own and was answered now reports that number and this board's own time
at the same moment**, as `repeaterClockS` and `agentClockS`. Two numbers and no arithmetic here;
the server stores the difference, draws it and says the last reading in words.

**It costs no request, no airtime and no transaction.** Nothing new goes on the air: the bytes
were already in a reply this firmware already waited for.

## What was built

- **`JobRunner::noteRepeaterClock`** (`lib/agent-core/src/JobRunner.cpp`), called from
  `awaitLoginReply` and from nowhere else. That is the whole of the rule: the wait only a **login
  reply** ends (task p1.10-47) is the one place a reply's four bytes are known to be a repeater's
  clock, so **whichever login it was** contributes — the job's own, the blank reachability probe
  between its two tries, the one after *no reply on a reused session*, and the discover's — and a
  late reply, another kind, or bytes `parseLoginReply` does not know never reaches it.
- **Three members on `mm::JobReport`**: `hasClock`, `repeaterClockS`, `agentClockS`, written
  **last of all** after `loggedInAs`, in `JobResultReport`'s own parameter order. One flag for the
  pair, because one of them alone is a `400` on `repeaterClockS`.
- **Three members on `JobRunner::Context`**, filled at the login and read at the report, so the
  reading survives whatever happens to the job after the login — as `loggedInAs` does.
- **The words.** `docs/library.md` §6.4 (a section of its own, with the three absences as a
  table) and §9 (the manifest commit), `CHANGELOG.md`'s `v0.7.1` (the four questions), and
  `release-notes/unreleased/53.md`.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The task's deliverable 2 says "a board whose own clock the server has not yet corrected
  reports none"; the gate implemented is `IClock::hasTime()`, which is any source** — SNTP, the
  server's time, or an RTC across a reset. Read literally, the task's wording would mean a board
  whose clock came off **SNTP** never reports a reading, and that contradicts the spec, which
  wins: EXIT-NODES §7 says the pair arrives "at every class a login is made at" and API §3 asks
  only for "what time the exit node thought it was", with the server bounding that number against
  its own clock within fifteen minutes. It is also unreachable in this firmware —
  `AgentClock`'s rule is that a time off the network **wins over** the server's, so a board with
  SNTP is never "corrected by the server" at all. The gate is therefore *has this board a time of
  its own*, which is the question the pair actually needs answered, and the server's own
  fifteen-minute bound is what judges how good that time was.
- **Nothing else.** The spec and the task agreed everywhere else they both spoke, and
  meshmaster.dk's `master` today (`461469e`) is what both were read from — API §3's
  `repeaterClockS` paragraph and EXIT-NODES §7's *What a login reply says besides yes*, both as
  task 210 merged them, plus report `tasks/phase-4.37/reports/210.md` for the contract's reasons.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The last answered login of the job wins** (deliverable 3's *say which is reported and why*).
  Keeping the reading in `awaitLoginReply` means a later answered login overwrites an earlier
  one, and that is the right way round: it is the freshest reading, and where a job made more than
  one it is the login the job went on to **run on**. In practice a job has at most one answered
  login — a fresh login is never re-tried, and the re-login after a reused session's silence
  happens only when there was no login of this job's before it — so the case this decides is the
  rotation path: a blank probe answered, then a second try answered, which reports the second.
- **The reachability probe's blank login counts**, although deliverable 3 names three logins and
  not four. It is a login of this job's and it was answered, so the four bytes are the repeater's
  clock exactly as any other login's are; excluding it would have meant a rule in
  `noteRepeaterClock` about *which* login, where the honest rule is *a login that was answered*.
  The case this reaches on its own is a job whose own tries were both silent and whose probe was
  not — `ASilenceRightAfterAnAnsweredBlankLoginIsReportedAsTheRefusalItIs` — which now carries a
  reading, and should: the repeater answered, and said what time it thought it was.
- **The reading says nothing in the job's log; it goes to the console.** This is the one decision
  that was taken twice. A line per answered login was written first, and it cost five other tests
  their evidence: `JobLog` is **1 024 bytes** and whole entries fall off the **front** when it
  fills, so a long job — a 34-line `config.read`, an `acl.set` confirmed by a blank login — lost
  the line that said what it did to a line saying again what the result already carries as two
  structured numbers. The job's log is a bounded shared resource and the server reads the fields,
  not the prose. So `LOG_I` says the two numbers and the difference in words, where a bench reads
  it, and the job's log is exactly as long as it was. The three new tests that check the sentence
  check it in `LogCapture`, and one of them asserts the job's log does **not** carry it.
- **The job's own log is where the two *absences* are explained, too — also on the console.** A
  job through the local door says so at **debug** (every job of a unit's own repeater does it, and
  at info it would be noise); a board with no clock of its own says so at **info**, because it is
  rare and it is the answer to *why is nothing reported*.
- **`answersLocally()` is the local door's test, asked at the moment the reply comes back.** It is
  true from a `beginJob` to its `endJob`, which is exactly the window a login happens in, and it
  is the same question `loginMaterialFor` already asks to decide that such a login carries nothing
  (task p1.10-41). The door answers the login itself, at admin, whatever it carried — so the four
  bytes are the door's and not a repeater's, and reporting them would put a number on the chart
  that no repeater ever said.
- **No plausibility bound on the repeater's number**, matching meshmaster.dk's decision: a
  repeater that starts again from January 2024 after a power loss is the thing this exists to see.
  The only bound is the one `uint32_t` already is, which is the range four bytes hold and the range
  the server accepts.
- **The two `followsShapeOf` assertions of a read a guest login served were widened into one
  helper, `followsBothFixtures`.** Such a result is the only one that carries `loggedInAs` **and**
  the clock pair, and no single fixture has both — `JobResultReportAtGuest.json` ends at
  `loggedInAs` and `JobResultReportWithClock.json` never carries one. The helper holds the body to
  each fixture with the other half cut out, so the order is still read off the fixtures and not
  typed into the test.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session can build, run the native tests and read the MeshCore source it builds against. It
cannot flash a board or hear a radio. With a board on the bench:

1. **Flash `heltec_v3` and run any job against a real repeater**, then read the console. One line
   per answered login: `jobs: job <id>: login (acl) try 1: the repeater's clock says <n> and ours
   <m>, which is <k> s ahead|behind; both go in the result`. The two numbers are Unix seconds; `k`
   is what meshmaster.dk will store.
2. **Check the result on meshmaster.dk**: the repeater's Overview should grow a **Clock** row with
   the last reading in words, and the Charts tab a *Clock offset* chart under System (its task
   210). A repeater this exit node has not logged in to since the flash shows neither.
3. **A repeater whose clock is actually wrong.** Power-cycle one without a battery-backed clock
   and read a job before anything sets it: the reading should be large and negative (the repeater
   behind), and it should step back to about zero after a `clock.sync`. That step is the picture
   the chart exists to draw.
4. **The fifteen-minute refusal is the server's and cannot be rehearsed here.** A report this
   board held offline for more than fifteen minutes — flight mode through a job, then a flush —
   loses its clock reading **and nothing else**, and the job's page says so. Worth seeing once so
   that the sentence is recognised when it appears.
5. **A job that reused a session carries no reading**, which on the bench is the second of two
   jobs for the same repeater inside the session lifetime: its result should have neither member
   and its console no clock line. The same for a `trace`, which logs in to nobody.
6. **The Walter's local door.** `answersLocally()` true means no reading for a unit's **own**
   repeater, which is this library's decision and takes effect at that firmware's next sync of
   `lib/agent-core`. Nothing on this board exercises it — the Heltec has no door — so the host
   test is all the evidence there is until then.
7. **A board whose clock is not set yet.** The first minutes after a cold boot with no SNTP and no
   heartbeat: a job that runs then reports neither number and says so at info. Rare, and the
   reason it is worth seeing is that *nothing reported* has two innocent causes and this is one of
   them.
