# p1.20-70 — A claim that was lost

The server leases the jobs of a claim **in the moment it answers**. On 21 November the Walter at
Bellevue claimed at 19:11Z, the answer came back *http 200* without its body, and the server had
leased it a download and a status read; the download has one attempt and was failed as *the exit
node took this job and never reported a result*. That unit counts 28 failed claims in 1 628
heartbeats, and `LostLease.cs` is meshmaster.dk telling the same story about 6 November.

After this task **every claim says what this exit node holds** — `holding`, `[]` when it holds
nothing — and **a claim that did not come back whole is made once more**, at once, in the same
wake. meshmaster.dk's task 254 answers such a claim with the jobs the first one lost, first and
with no attempt spent. And a job handed over again whose result this end keeps is **not run
twice**: the kept result is posted.

**What a wake's log says now**, where it said nothing about a lost claim before:

```
[19:11:04] W jobs: the claim's answer did not come back whole (http 200 but the claim did not parse: …):
               the server may have leased jobs this exit node knows nothing of, so it is asked once more
[19:11:52] I wake: 1 claim(s), then the wake's claims, 2 job(s) run, 0 not run, …
[19:11:52] I wake: 1 claim(s) whose answer did not come back whole and were asked once more
```

## What was built

1. **`holding` in the writer.** `mm::writeClaimRequest` gained `(const long long* holding, int
   holdingCount)` and writes the member **last**, after `transactionsLeft` (API §3's order).
   `holdingCount` **below 0 is not stated** — *this agent does not say* — and **0 writes `[]`** —
   *nothing held*; the two are different things to the server and the writer keeps them apart.
   `mm::kClaimHoldingMax` (8) is its bound and `mm::kMaxClaimRequestLen` (256) is the longest body
   it can make, which is what the uplink sizes its request buffer by.
2. **`JobRunner::holding(out, cap)` and `holdsResultFor(id)`**, and `claim()` fills the member for
   every claim it makes. The runner owns the result buffer, so this is the one place the ids are
   read and no caller can forget to pass them. A `static_assert` ties `kClaimHoldingMax` to
   `ResultBuffer::kMaxResults`, so the member always says everything this end holds.
3. **`mm::answerWasLost(reachable, httpStatus)`** in `agent/ServerPolicy.h`, beside `actionFor`: a
   pure function of what came back, true for no reply at all and for a 2xx this client could not
   read, false for everything the server spoke. It lives there because that file is where *what one
   answer means* lives, and `test_server_policy` holds it status by status with no board.
4. **One more claim in `wakeJobs`.** A claim that came back lost is made again at once, with the
   same `max` and the same `holding`, while there is uplink time for it. The first failure counts
   **nothing** — not a `claimFailures`, not a `noteFailure`, not a consecutive failure — because the
   second claim is the one that decides; if it fails, that one is counted and `noteFailure` is
   called with *its* answer, which is how a 401 on the second attempt still halts.
5. **The door in `wakeRunClaimed`**, beside the mesh's: a job this end keeps a result for is asked
   about **before the job is priced**, for the same reason `meshDeclines` is — nothing of it goes on
   the air, so there is no price for a bound to refuse.
6. **The short circuit in `runClaimed`**, which is the one place that decides it for both ways of
   being driven: the credential is wiped unused, nothing goes on the air, and `flushResults()`
   posts the kept result through the same budget as every other post.
7. **`WakeReport::claimsLost` and `jobsHandedOverAgain`**, and two log lines at the end of a wake
   that are printed only when they happened.
8. **A claim-shaped fault in the host fake.** `FakeUplink::loseTheAnswerToClaims(n)` leases the
   claim's jobs, forgets to answer, and **hands them back to the next claim that says it does not
   hold them** — which is meshmaster.dk's task 254's half, so the test of 21 November is a test
   against the behaviour the two ends agreed on and not against a bool. `failClaims(fault, n)` is
   the refusal beside it.
9. **The two waiting fixtures**, their rows in `tests/fixtures/contracts-ahead/README.md`, and
   their lines in `kWaiting`.
10. **The words:** `docs/library.md` §4 (the `claimJobs` row, with what `lastCall()` must say),
    §6 (the three paragraphs of this task) and §9.1 (four wait now); `docs/host.md` (a fourth thing
    a test of a wake is about, the fake's new faults, the wake's one-line shape);
    `CHANGELOG.md` v0.7.6 (the method's new parameters and what they buy);
    `release-notes/unreleased/70.md`.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **`holding` is filled inside `JobRunner::claim` rather than passed in by the caller.** The task
  says *every* claim this library writes carries it, and the runner is the one object that owns the
  buffer the ids come from; making it a parameter of `claim()` would have let a caller write a claim
  without it, which is the one thing the contract forbids of an agent that writes it at all. The
  cost is that `claim()`'s signature did not change at all, so task 69's callers are unedited.
- **"Once more" is per claim, not per wake.** A wake of four claims whose second answer is lost asks
  that one again and carries on; it is only a *second* failure of the same claim that ends the
  wake's jobs, and since that ends them there is never a third attempt at anything. Reading it as
  *one retry a wake* would have made the fourth claim of a wake less robust than the first for no
  reason the task gives.
- **The first failure counts nothing and calls `noteFailure` on nothing.** The alternative — count
  it, then uncount it if the second succeeds — is a counter that goes up and down, and the question
  `claimFailures` answers is *how many claims this unit could not make*, which a pair that came back
  is not one of. The backoff is the same argument: a wake that got its work done owes the server no
  wait.
- **`answerWasLost` is a pure function in `ServerPolicy.h`, not a method on `Call`.** It is the same
  kind of thing as `actionFor` and `saysWhenToComeBack` — what one answer *means* — and putting it
  there is what lets `test_server_policy` walk every status code without a loop, a fake or a board.
- **A 2xx is "lost" and a 3xx is not.** The function is only ever asked of a call that *failed*, so
  a 2xx reaching it is a 2xx whose body the client could not read — the server acted and this end
  does not know what it did. A 3xx is an answer the server composed deliberately and this client
  does not follow redirects, so it is a refusal like any other.
- **A job handed over again is neither `jobsRun` nor `jobsNotRun`.** `jobsRun` would be a lie and
  `jobsNotRun`'s own comment is *claimed, never on the air: reported `failed`, wake budget*, which
  this is not. It has a counter of its own on both the report and the runner, and the `jobs` step is
  `Done` because something was dealt with.
- **"Its kept result is posted" is `flushResults()` and not a post of that one result.** The buffer
  is posted oldest first and `popOldest` is how a result leaves it; posting out of turn would break
  that order for the sake of one job. The flush respects the byte cap and the uplink seconds like
  every other post, so a budget that cannot carry it keeps it for the next wake — and `holding` goes
  on saying so.
- **A request that does not fit is refused by the client as a 400.** `kMaxClaimRequestLen` is sized
  so that the writer never has to refuse one — `test_claim` compiles the longest body there is and
  checks it fits — but the branch exists, and giving it `reachable` true with `httpStatus` 400 says
  the truth: nothing went on the wire, so there is no lost answer and no lease, and `Discard` is the
  right action for a body that would only be wrong again.
- **The fake server stands in for meshmaster.dk's task 254.** Writing the test of 21 November
  without it would have meant asserting that a second claim *went out* and nothing about the jobs
  coming back, which is half the story; the fake implements the documented rule (hand back every
  lease this exit node did not name, first and before anything new) so the test is about the
  behaviour the two ends agreed on.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot flash a board or hear a radio. Three of these four are the server's or the
Walter's and come after this release.

1. **A Heltec in the field, with nothing of the server's half deployed.** Flash it and watch
   `server` and `jobs` on the console through a claim: the claim is made at the poll as before, one
   a pass, and the one thing that changed is its body — `{"max":2,"holding":[]}` instead of
   `{"max":2}`. Nothing a person sees on the console changes, and nothing should.
2. **The server's half is not deployed.** meshmaster.dk's pile 4.47 must be merged and deployed
   before this is released: its task 253 is on `master` now, its **task 254 is not**, and 254 is
   what hands a lease back on `holding`. Until it is there this firmware sends an optional member an
   older server ignores — safe, and exactly today's behaviour — and the lost lease is still the
   server's sweep to clear.
3. **The claim that did not come back, on the Walter**, after this release, its sync and its pile 18
   (which is what sets `maxClaimsThisWake`): the unit's *agent calls* list with a claim in it that
   did not come back, the `W jobs: the claim's answer did not come back whole …` line after it, and
   the job of that wake run all the same. `loop()` makes no second claim, so a Heltec cannot show
   this at all — its next poll is the second claim.
4. **The fixtures are reconciled after that sync** — the first item of the pile's `FOLLOWUPS.md`.
   The four files have now been compared with meshmaster.dk's generated ones by hand and are
   identical, so the sync should be a formality: `.\tools\sync-fixtures.ps1 -Commit master`, the
   manifest commit moves, the tests are pointed at the copied tree and the four waiting files go.
