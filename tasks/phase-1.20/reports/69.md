# p1.20-69 — A wake claims again

A claim takes two jobs, because two is the room this firmware has at a time, and a wake was one
claim — so a unit that sleeps did two jobs an hour at best, whatever was queued. On 21 November
that spread a NoiseScope sample from the Walter at Bellevue over five wakes, and four times a day
the scheduled `neighbours` listing takes one of the two places and that hour's `telemetry` expires
unread.

After this a wake that has finished what it was handed **asks again**, while all six of these
hold: the answer to the last result it posted said `jobsWaiting`; no result of that claim was kept
back for the budget; the last claim handed over at least one job; the transaction cap can still
hold a job; there is uplink time left; and it has made fewer than `maxClaimsThisWake` claims. The
number of claims is the caller's and **is not sent to the server** — it is the board that knows
its battery — and a caller that says nothing gets one claim: what every wake made before.

**Nothing on a Heltec changes.** `loop()` is untouched, its claim is still `{"max":2}` to the
byte, and it does nothing with `jobsWaiting`. The Walter turns this on in its own pile 18.

**What the wake's log says now**, where it said nothing about claims before:

```
[12:03:41] I jobs: the server says more waits and this wake has room: claiming again (2 of 4)
[12:03:52] I jobs: 4 claim(s) this wake and no more: a result of the last one is kept for the next wake
[12:03:52] I wake: 4 claim(s), then a result kept, 7 job(s) run, 0 not run, 9 transaction(s), …
```

## What was built

1. **`WakeIntent::maxClaimsThisWake`** (`agent/AgentLoop.h`). 0 and 1 are one claim; a larger
   number is the most this wake makes. Not sent to the server.
2. **`wakeJobs` is a loop** (`agent-core/src/AgentLoop.cpp`). The claim and its jobs, and then
   again while the six conditions hold. Every time round reads the uplink's seconds and
   `claimMax()` at the top, where the first claim read them, so **every claim is priced as the
   first is**; `wakeRunClaimed` is the jobs of one claim, lifted out unchanged so that there is one
   place they run from. Before the first claim the `claim` step's own outcome says why none was
   made (`NoTime`, `NoBudget`, `Failed`), as it always did; after one the step is `Done` and
   `report.claimsEndedBy` says what ended the asking.
3. **`WakeReport::claimsMade` and `claimsEndedBy`**, and `jobsClaimed` counted over the whole wake
   (`jobsRun` and `jobsNotRun` already accumulated). The wake's one-line log carries the claims and
   the reason.
4. **`transactionsLeft` in the later claims.** `mm::writeClaimRequest` gained a fourth argument and
   writes the member after `max`; **below 0 is not stated**, which is the first claim of a wake,
   every claim `loop()` makes, and every claim of a wake whose intent proposed no cap.
5. **`jobsWaiting` carried up.** `mm::resultReplySaysJobsWaiting(body, len)` is the one reader of
   the result's answer; `IUplink::postJobResult` gained `bool& jobsWaiting` and the uplink fills it
   from the body it already has, so the reply is parsed once and the library is handed an answer
   rather than the bytes. `JobRunner::lastResultSaidJobsWaiting()` is what the wake reads, and
   `claim()` clears it so that it is always about the claim being finished. `loop()` does nothing
   with it.
6. **The two waiting fixtures**, their rows in `tests/fixtures/contracts-ahead/README.md`, and
   their lines in `kWaiting`.
7. **The words:** `docs/library.md` §4 (the two methods) and §6 (the wake, with the six
   conditions), §9.1 (two wait again), `docs/host.md` (what a test of a wake is about),
   `CHANGELOG.md` v0.7.6 (the two methods that moved, and the one line each a consumer writes to
   follow them), `release-notes/unreleased/69.md`.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **`IUplink`'s two methods changed signature rather than gaining defaulted siblings.** The
  precedent for not breaking a consumer is `postUpdateResult`, which was added *with a body*
  (p1.15-61) — but that was a whole new call a consumer may not want, while these two are the same
  two calls with one more thing to say each, and a second overload of each would have put two names
  on one route for ever. So the interface stays one method per route and `CHANGELOG.md` answers its
  first question with *yes*: it is one line each in an existing implementation, and a consumer that
  does nothing but follow the signatures keeps exactly today's behaviour.
- **`transactionsLeft` below 0 means "not stated"** rather than a separate `has…` flag. The value
  is an integer of 0 or more on the wire, so a negative number cannot be confused with one, and it
  keeps `writeClaimRequest`'s existing call site working unchanged.
- **The order the six conditions are read in**, which decides what `claimsEndedBy` says when more
  than one is true: the wake's claims, then a kept result, then `jobsWaiting`, then — at the top of
  the next turn — the uplink's seconds and the transactions. A kept result and spent uplink seconds
  usually arrive together, and *a result kept* is the more useful of the two sentences.
- **`claim()` clears the flag** rather than the wake resetting it. The question is always "what did
  the answer to the last result of *this* claim say", so the one place that starts a claim is the
  one place that forgets the last one's answer; nothing stale can be read.
- **`claimsEndedBy` is a static string, like `WakeReport::why`**, and `""` when no claim was made
  at all — then the `claim` step's outcome is the answer and a second sentence would contradict it.
- **A failed later claim is not a backoff of its own kind**: it counts a `claimFailures`, calls
  `noteFailure` and ends the wake's jobs exactly as a failed first claim does, and
  `claimsEndedBy` says *the claim did not come back*. (Task 70 owns making a claim again.)
- **The fake server answers a result with the contract's own bytes**: `FakeUplink` writes a
  `JobResultReply` body and runs it through `mm::resultReplySaysJobsWaiting`, so a test of a wake
  that claims again is a test against those bytes and not against a bool a fake set.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot flash a board or hear a radio, and **nothing of this changes a Heltec** — it never
calls `wake()`. The list is therefore short and most of it is the Walter's pile 18, after this
release and the Walter's sync:

1. **A Heltec is unchanged in the field.** Flash it and watch `server` and `jobs` on the console
   through a claim: the claim is made at the poll as before, one a pass, and the wake log line is
   not printed at all because `loop()` is not a wake.
2. **A wake of several claims on the Walter**, once its pile 18 sets `maxClaimsThisWake`: the
   serial console prints `jobs: the server says more waits and this wake has room: claiming again
   (N of M)` between the claims and one `wake: N claim(s), then <reason>, …` line at the end, so the
   walk is reading those two lines and comparing them with the unit's *Jobs* tab.
3. **The server's half is not deployed.** meshmaster.dk's pile 4.47 must be merged and deployed
   before this is released, because a server that never writes `jobsWaiting` in a result's answer
   leaves every wake at one claim — which is safe and is exactly today's behaviour, but means the
   feature does nothing until it is there. Nothing this firmware sends is new to an older server:
   `transactionsLeft` is an optional member a wake writes only in a claim after its first, and the
   first claim is unchanged to the byte.
4. **The fixtures are reconciled after that sync** — the first item of the pile's `FOLLOWUPS.md`:
   `.\tools\sync-fixtures.ps1 -Commit master`, the four files compared byte for byte with the
   waiting ones, the tests pointed at the copied tree, the waiting files deleted.
