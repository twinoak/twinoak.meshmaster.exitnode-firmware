# p1.3-12 — The small ones

Four boxes, and the one with teeth is the fourth. **The display tells the truth
about two states it used to lie about**: a board with WiFi up and no token reads
`no token: set it` rather than `Idle, no jobs yet` under `Online: no`, and a
`Retry-After` hold reads `no server: retry 45s` under `Online: no` rather than
`Idle` under `Online: yes`. **The config read takes the list meshmaster.dk is
about to send it** — thirty-two keys where it refused twenty-one, twelve raw
lines where six were dropped in silence, and a result writer that drops raw
lines before it ever reports a read `failed`. And `boards/README.md` says why
`otadata` stays where the framework put it.

```
ID: 46F6     12:03:14        a board after the first merged flash: WiFi up,
WiFi: yes Online: no         no token, and line four says which of the two
Heartbeat: none              things to do next
no token: set it
```

## The sizes

What the three constants cost, measured. `kMaxConfigKeys` 20 → 32,
`kMaxConfigRawLines` 6 → 12 (and one slot beyond them for the count of what did
not fit, so the array is 13), `kDataCap` 4 608 → 8 192, `kBodyCap` 6 656 →
10 240.

| | before | after | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ConfigSnapshot` (`g_snapshot`) | 6 536 | **10 852** | `values[]` 7 744 of it: 32 slots × 242 bytes (a 32-character key, a 208-character value, a NUL each). `raw[]` 2 613: 13 × 201. `acl` 346. |
| `ClaimedJob` (×2 in `g_jobs`) | 6 208 | **6 592** on the board, 6 608 on the host | `configKeys[]` 1 056 of it: 32 × 33 |
| `g_data`, the result | 4 608 | **8 192** | one job's result JSON |
| `g_body`, the POST | 6 656 | **10 240** | the result, the envelope and the 1 KB job log |
| `ResultBuffer::kCapacityBytes` | 12 288 | **12 288**, unchanged | the offline arena, 8 results; untouched by this task |

`+4 316 + 384×2 + 3 584 + 3 584 = 12 252`, and the V3's RAM grew by 12 264 —
the twelve are alignment. The V3 is at 48.9 % of its 320 KB with 167 KB free for
the TLS handshake's heap, which is where the headroom has to be.

**Why `kDataCap` is 8 192 and not the worst case the constants allow.** The
unescaped ceiling of those bounds is about **10.9 KB** (32 values at 240
characters, the ACL at 344, twelve raw lines at 200, the two extras,
`aclRestored`), and `JsonWriter` escapes as `JavaScriptEncoder.Default` does, so
one byte can become six (`\u001F`): a buffer that could never overflow would be
some 65 KB, which is a fifth of the board's RAM spent on a repeater that
answers 208 characters to every one of 32 keys. 8 192 is sized for the read
that will actually arrive — the Configuration tab's twenty-six keys, a 119-
character `owner.info`, the regions listing, a full ACL and twelve raw lines is
about 4.2 KB, which 4 608 held only just — and `writeConfigReadResult`
degrades above it rather than failing. `static_assert(kDataCap >=
kMinConfigReadResultCap)` and
`static_assert(kBodyCap > kDataCap + sizeof(JobLog))` hold the two relations a
future change could break.

**How it degrades.** `writeConfigReadResult` writes the whole snapshot first —
which is the attempt the contract fixture is compared byte for byte against, so
nothing about the normal result changed — and when that overflows it drops raw
lines one at a time, and only when none are left does it drop trailing values.
Whatever went is said as one more raw line:
`result: N value(s) and M raw line(s) left out, it did not fit 8192 bytes`. So a
long `owner.info` costs a raw line, never the read, and
`runConfigRead`'s `dataLen > 0 ? Ok : Failed` cannot reach `Failed` for want of
room any more. `ConfigReadFullList.ABufferTooSmallCostsRawLinesAndThenValuesNeverTheRead`
is the proof: a snapshot at the full ceiling into 8 192 keeps all 32 values and
loses the raw lines (`0 value(s)` in the note), and the same snapshot into
`kMinConfigReadResultCap` still parses, with the cut note as its one raw line.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The task offers two ways to size `kDataCap` and this took both halves of
  the second.** "Size it from the constants … with a `static_assert`, or make
  the writer drop raw lines first and say so in the JSON". Sizing it from the
  constants is 65 KB once escaping is counted (the paragraph above), so the
  writer degrades — and `kDataCap` was raised anyway, to 8 192, because twelve
  raw lines instead of six is 1.2 KB more in the realistic case and 4 608 had
  about 400 bytes of room. The `static_assert`s hold the floor and the body's
  relation to the data rather than the unreachable ceiling.
- **The 32-key read is tested at the snapshot and the writer, not "against the
  fake".** The task says "One native test reads a claim of 32 keys against the
  fake with three unknown keys …". The fake repeater is task p1.1-08's host
  harness and that branch is still stopped
  (`tasks/phase-1.1/logs/STOPPED-08.md`), so there is no fake in this tree to
  read against. The test builds the snapshot a V3 at v1.17 would have made from
  exactly those answers and holds the result, which is where every byte of a
  `config.read` result is decided; `JobRunner::runConfigRead`'s loop over the
  air is the part that is not covered, and it is unchanged by this task but for
  the constants it reads. 08's resume should re-run this list over the fake.
- **No native test for `online()`, as the task's own fallback allows.**
  `AgentLoop` has no native seam (see the second checklist item).
- **The spec's §3 "as built" paragraph is a key behind its own code, and that
  is meshmaster.dk's to fix.** `spec/CONFIGURATION.md` §3 says "the list is
  fifteen keys, the cost is eighteen transactions"; §2's own paragraph on
  `agc.reset.interval` says it is the fourteenth key of the default read list,
  and `ConfigReadPayload.DefaultKeys` has sixteen entries, so the cost is
  nineteen and `spec/EXIT-NODES.md` §7 ("sixteen keys … 19 + login = 20") agrees
  with the code. This firmware follows the code and §2 — sixteen keys, 19
  transactions — and the stale sentence is one line of §3 in the other
  repository. Noted for the reviewer; nothing here depends on it.
- **`p4.9-102` does not exist yet.** `tasks/phase-4.9/` is not in
  meshmaster.dk at today's `master` (the piles there stop at 4.8), so the
  "about twenty-six keys" list could not be read and pinned. The cap of 32 is
  the task's number and is 23 % over that estimate; if the list comes out longer
  than 32 the refusal sentence is what the server will see, which is the right
  failure.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **`NoToken` says `no token: set it` and not `no server set`.** The predicate
  is `AgentClient::configured()`, which is a URL *and* a token — but
  `meshmaster.url` falls back to `https://meshmaster.dk` when unset, so the only
  way to be unconfigured is to have no token. The line names the thing to do.
- **The order is `TokenRefused`, `NoWifi`, `NoToken`, `Backoff`**, which is the
  task's wording taken literally. The reasoning is written into
  `DisplayLines.h`: below *no wifi* because a board with no WiFi cannot use a
  token yet, above the backoff because an unprovisioned board never reaches one
  (`AgentLoop::loop` returns early on `!server_->configured()`, so the two can
  only meet after a token is cleared mid-flight).
- **`online()` reads `holdUntil_` through `elapsed()` and not
  `retrySeconds() > 0`.** `retrySeconds()` is whole seconds, so a hold of
  400 ms would round to 0 and leave `online()` true for a wait that is still
  running. The panel's `backingOff` uses `retrySeconds() > 0` because that is
  what the task asks for and what the countdown prints; the sub-second
  difference between the two is invisible on a panel redrawn once a second.
- **The overflow line counts lines, and does not keep the thirteenth.** There
  is nowhere to put 200 more characters, so `addRaw` keeps the twelve it has,
  counts what it refused, and writes the count into the slot after them. The
  alternative — overwriting the twelfth with the note — would lose a line the
  repeater did say to record one it did not.
- **The cut note is a raw line and not a field of its own.**
  `ConfigReadResult` is a contract record (`Values`, `Raw`, `Firmware`, `Board`,
  `AclRestored`) and a new field would be a change to it, which is the other
  repository's. `raw` is already "anything the agent could not parse, kept
  verbatim so the server never loses information", and "what did not fit" is the
  same kind of thing. The note's text is distinctive (`result: …`) and the
  server stores raw lines rather than parsing them, so it costs nothing there.
- **`ResultBuffer` was not touched.** A maximal body is 10 240 now, so the
  12 288-byte arena holds one of them where it used to hold one of 6 656 — it
  never held two maximal bodies. What it does hold is two *real* `config.read`
  results, which is what its comment claims and which is still true at about
  4.5 KB each. Growing the arena would have been RAM spent on an outage that
  coincides with two pathological reads.
- **The read's cost in `docs/console.md` moved from 18/20 to 19/21** and the
  sample log line's `20 transaction(s)` to `21`, because the list is a key
  longer. `AgentLoop.h`'s "a config.read of twenty transactions … is ten
  minutes" is "twenty-one … eleven minutes" for the same reason.

## Not verified here (and why)

A session cannot light a panel, make a server answer 503, or put a repeater on
the air.

1. **`no token: set it` on the glass.** The state is the one the first merged
   flash of task p1.2-11 leaves, so it comes free with bench step 2 of pile
   1.2's walk: flash `-merged.bin`, let WiFi come up (`set wifi.ssid`,
   `set wifi.pass`), and before setting the token the panel must read
   `WiFi: yes Online: no` / `Heartbeat: none` / `no token: set it`. Then
   `set meshmaster.token <token>` and line four should become
   `Idle, no jobs yet` within a poll interval, and line three a time.
2. **`online()` and the hold, through a real deploy.** The next release of
   meshmaster.dk is the test, with `pio device monitor -b 115200` open. If the
   server's 503 carries `Retry-After`, the log says `server: http 503; the wait
   is Retry-After's N s` and the panel must read `Online: no` and
   `no server: retry Ns` counting down — and must go back to `Online: yes` and
   `Idle, …` on the first answered heartbeat after it, with
   `errors` showing `httpFailures` moved and **`0 in a row`**, which is the
   whole point of leaving the counters alone. If the 503 carries no header the
   board backs off instead and the panel reads the same thing from the backoff,
   which it already did. (Task p1.2-10's own note stands: if the deploy puts no
   `Retry-After` on its 503s, that is one line in meshmaster.dk's proxy.)
3. **A `config.read` of the full list over the air.** Needs meshmaster.dk's
   task p4.9-102 shipped and a repeater on the bench. **Flash the Heltec with
   this pile's build before that release**, or the daily read ends
   `unsupported`— the refusal sentence — until then (the pile's finding 5). What
   to watch when it does run: `jobs` says `ok after 31 transaction(s)` for a
   26-key list (26 + 2 extras + 1 for `regions` is 29, plus the login and the
   ACL restore; the default sixteen are 21 the same way), line four reads
   `Job <id> config.read` for the length of it, the job's page shows a `raw`
   array with one line per key the V3 does not know (the FEM gains among them)
   and **no** `result: … left out` line, and the Configuration tab shows
   `agc.reset.interval` as read rather than unknown. A read that ends `failed`
   with an empty `data` is the one failure this task exists to prevent.
4. **The lease, at the new length.** 32 keys is 35 transactions and at the
   default budget of two a minute that is about eighteen minutes of wall clock.
   `JobRunner::pumpDuringAJob` never waits past `leaseUntil` and logs *the
   budget's next slot is past the lease* when it would, and the server's lease
   is computed from the same `ConfigReadPayload.Transactions` arithmetic — so
   the two should agree, and the thing to confirm on the first full read is that
   it ends `ok` rather than stopping mid-list. If it stops, the lease is the
   server's to lengthen, not this firmware's to ignore.
5. **`otadata` after a merged flash.** Already proven in the file (above), but
   the behaviour it protects is bench step 2 and 3 of pile 1.2's walk: the board
   must boot `app0` after a merged flash with no `Failed to initialize NVS` and
   no second-slot confusion.
