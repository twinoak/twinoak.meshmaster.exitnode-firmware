# p1.6-22 — What a sleep keeps

A result the server would not take — or one a wake kept for its budget, which
21 made the normal answer to a tight cap — waited in the offline buffer, and the
offline buffer is RAM. On a Heltec that is the whole story. On a unit that
deep-sleeps between wakes the RAM goes with the sleep: the next boot had nothing
to post and the job stayed leased until the server's lease lapsed, so *no job
left leased and silent* held only within one boot; the adverts a wake could not
post went the same way. `WakeState` cannot carry them (twelve kilobytes of
results against an RTC memory with 36 bytes free), so this task gives a sleeping
consumer a **second, optional seam** — `agent/IRetained.h`, three methods: a
capacity, write one image, read it back — and the loop writes **one image a
wake** into it, the kept results first and then the ring's waiting adverts,
whole or not at all, versioned and checksummed, and reads it back after the next
boot. What the store cannot hold is dropped and **counted** in the two figures
the heartbeat already carries, and both figures travel in the image, so a loss
is a number on the exit node's page and not an accident.

The Heltec passes none: `loop()` never reaches the store, nothing of the new
code is linked into its image (`nm` finds no symbol of it, and none of its log
text is in the binary), and every existing native case passes unchanged. **Its
RAM grew by 304 bytes** — 256 of them the one struct field this needed, an
advert's epoch, in a 64-entry ring — and that is named below, byte by byte.

`pio test -e native`: **370 cases in 22 suites, all passing**, in the container
`RUNBOOK.md` §2 names. Both boards SUCCESS with `-Werror`. The host matrix,
the component check and the fixture check are green too.

## The seam, the image, the rule

| | |
|---|---|
| `agent/IRetained.h` | **new.** `capacity()`, `write(image, len)`, `read(out, cap)`. The header says what the loop does with it, what the image may hold (nothing a result does not), and what it costs in wear |
| `agent/KeptImage.h`, `src/KeptImage.cpp` | **new.** The image's layout as bytes — little-endian, written down field by field — a `KeptImageWriter` (results first, then adverts, each whole or refused, `finish()` writes the header and the CRC) and a `KeptImageReader` (`check()` holds the whole image before anything is read: header, magic, version, length against what was read, CRC-32, then every record's bounds against the length and against the buffer and ring it will go into). `crc32Of` is CRC-32/ISO-HDLC, the one zlib and the ESP32 ROM compute |

| `CHANGELOG.md` | `## v0.3.0 (unreleased)` opened, with this task's lines and the lines 20 and 21 left for it |

**The image** (`agent/KeptImage.h` has it field by field): a 32-byte header —
magic `KEPT`, CRC-32 over everything after itself, version 1, the two counts,
the length, and three counters: `resultsDropped`, `observationsDropped`,
`imagesRefused` — then each result as `i64 jobId, u32 len, body[len]`, the
body exactly what `postJobResult` would be handed; then each advert as a fixed
108-byte record with the epoch it was heard. No field for anything else.

**The rule** (`docs/library.md` §5.1 says it in one sentence): *kept results
first, oldest first, then the waiting adverts with the moment each was heard,
whole or not at all, one write a wake and none when nothing changed.* Results
first because a lost result leaves a job leased and a lost advert is heard
again. The writer stops at the first result that does not fit and at the first
advert that does not fit; what it stopped at and everything behind it is
dropped from the buffer and the ring too, counted once, and said once — so the
RAM agrees with the image and `resultsDropped` means what the heartbeat says.

**Wear.** The loop keeps the checksum of the image it last read or wrote and
writes nothing when the next one would be the same — which covers *nothing kept
and the store already empty*, because an empty image with the same counters is
the same bytes. A store the loop has not looked at (no `restoreKept()`) or
whose write failed is unknown and is written at the next wake whatever it would
hold. `AWakeWritesAtMostOneImageAndNoneWhenTheStoreAlreadyHoldsIt` counts the
store's write attempts through nine wakes: 0 for nothing, 1 for a kept result,
still 1 through two more metered wakes, one attempt refused and one retried,
one for the empty image after everything posted, and none after that.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The loop is lent a scratch as well as a store.** The task says *no
  allocation in the library* and *write one image*, and an image is up to about
  19.5 kB that has to exist somewhere before one call can write it whole. So
  `setRetained(store, scratch, scratchLen)`: the RAM is the consumer's, sized to
  what it wants kept, and the image is never larger than the smaller of the
  scratch and the store's capacity. The alternative — a store written in pieces
  at offsets — would have put *whole or not at all* on the consumer and made an
  NVS blob impossible to implement without the same buffer on their side.
- **What the store cannot hold is dropped from RAM, not only counted.** The task
  says *counted in the counters the heartbeat already has and logged once*. It is,
  and the result or advert is also popped from the buffer or the ring at the
  write, because the image is what survives and a figure that said *dropped*
  about something still in RAM would be wrong on a unit that did not sleep after
  all, and counted twice on one that kept it for a second wake. The buffer and
  the ring agree with the image; a consumer that hands a store is a consumer
  that sleeps, and the header of `IRetained.h` says so.
- **The two dropped counters survive the sleep in the image, not in
  `WakeState`.** They are the buffer's and the ring's own (`resultsDropped()`,
  `ring_.dropped()`), reset at every boot today, and a loss the store caused
  would have been forgotten by the next heartbeat. The image's header carries
  them (and the refused-image count), `restoreKept()` adds them back, and
  `WakeState::kVersion` did not move — which the Walter's retained slot cares
  about.
- **`HeardAdvert` gained a field**, `heardAtEpoch`, and the Heltec's ring is
  256 bytes larger for it. *The Heltec passes none and is unchanged, byte for
  byte* is therefore true of its behaviour, its heartbeat and every existing
  test, and not of its RAM. The alternatives were all worse: an age in
  milliseconds cannot be re-based on a boot whose wall clock is unset until the
  heartbeat answers (the Walter with no RTC, which is the common case), and a
  side array in the loop costs the same 256 bytes with a second structure to
  keep in step. The mesh never sets the field and the batch reads it only when
  it is non-zero, so the Heltec's batches are the bytes they were.
- **`restoreKept()` is the sibling, not `restore()`.** The task allowed either.
  `restore()` refuses a `WakeState` of another version and changes nothing; an
  image is a different object with a different version, and a boot after a
  firmware update can have one of them refused and the other read. Two calls,
  each honest about its own return value, and `docs/library.md` §6 puts them one
  after the other.
- **The credentials test runs the real rotation with the .NET agent's test
  passwords and the fixtures' samples where they fit.** The contract fixtures'
  three samples are 19 to 21 characters, and this firmware keeps fifteen
  (`kMaxPasswordLen`, stock firmware's `char password[16]`): a rotation *to*
  `new-password-sample` is refused as a lock-out before anything is sent, which
  the test includes as one of its five, and a login with `admin-password-sample`
  is a login the repeater will not answer. The rotation the repeater confirms
  uses `hunter2` → `Rotated-Admin-9`, the passwords of
  `MeshMaster.Agent.Tests`' `CredentialTests` that `test_credentials` already
  holds; the guest login uses the fixtures' `guest-password-sample`, which both
  ends cut to the same fifteen bytes and so answers. All five, and the fifteen-
  byte prefix of each, are what the image is grepped for.
- **Nothing of meshmaster.dk disagreed.** `spec/EXIT-NODES.md` §5 was read at
  today's `origin/master` (**`3ef1a79`**, pile 4.15 merged). *Offline is normal*
  and *the server de-duplicates on the client id* are what the kept image leans
  on. One sentence of §5's *What it cannot do* — *the offline buffer is a RAM
  ring and not a journal, so a board that loses power loses what it had not
  posted* — is now true of the Heltec and of any consumer that passes no store,
  and not of one that does. The protocol is unchanged, so there is nothing for
  the other repository beyond that sentence, which the reviewer may want to
  narrow to the board it describes.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **The image is bytes, little-endian, written down field by field**, and not
  a struct copied — a unit lives with this layout across thousands of sleeps and
  a firmware update, and a struct's padding and `size_t` are the compiler's and
  the target's. The CRC covers everything after itself, so a torn header is a
  bad magic or a bad checksum and never a checksum over its own stale value.
- **CRC-32/ISO-HDLC, bitwise, no table.** It is what zlib's `crc32()` and the
  ESP32 ROM's `esp_rom_crc32_le()` compute, so a consumer can check an image with
  what it already has; a 1 kB table for a few kilobytes once a wake was not worth
  its rodata on the board that does not link this anyway.
- **The writer stops at the first result that does not fit**, rather than
  skipping it for a smaller one behind it: the buffer is a queue and the image is
  its order, and a result dropped from the middle would be a buffer that could no
  longer be read oldest-first. The adverts the same.
- **A wake that sent nothing still writes** (halted, link down, a wait still
  running) if the image would differ — the ring can gain adverts at any time the
  mesh pumps — and does not if it would not. The checksum comparison is the one
  rule for both.
- **`restoreKept()` twice in a boot reads nothing the second time**, with a
  warning: the same image pushed twice would post every result twice and the
  server would de-duplicate the results but not the batches.
- **A store the loop has not looked at is written once whatever the image would
  hold.** A consumer that forgets `restoreKept()` gets a fresh, consistent image
  at its first wake rather than a stale one left from before an update.
- **`heard()` and `held()` are not carried** — the console's *heard in all* and
  *held in all* start again at a boot, as every console figure of a unit that
  sleeps does; the two *dropped* counters are carried because the heartbeat
  reports them.
- **The reader refuses an image whose counts the buffer or the ring could not
  hold**, and whose adverts carry a path longer than `kMaxObservationPathBytes`
  or a hash size above three, as *its records do not add up* — a layout change
  that moved a bound is a version bump, and a version bump is refused by name.
- **`restoreKept()` into a buffer or ring that is not empty appends and
  warns.** The documented order is `begin()`, `setRetained()`, `restore()`,
  `restoreKept()`, then the first wake; a consumer that ran a wake first gets its
  older results behind the newer ones and a line saying so.
- **The scratch's minimum is a header.** A store or scratch smaller than 32
  bytes is *no store*, with one error line at `setRetained`, rather than a store
  that refuses every image silently.
- **Names.** `IRetained` (the task's own suggestion, and the Walter's word for
  the same idea — `interfaces::Retained`), `KeptImage` for the format (*kept* is
  the word 21 gave a result the budget could not carry, and *what a sleep keeps*
  is the task), `setRetained` / `restoreKept` beside `restore`, and
  `keptImageWritten` in the report.
- **`FakeRetained` counts write attempts, not successes**, because *at most one
  image a wake* is about attempts, and a refused write is still a write the
  store was asked for.
- **`CHANGELOG.md`'s `v0.3.0 (unreleased)` section carries 20's and 21's lines
  as well as this task's.** Both reports said the section they could not open
  *wants these lines* and that their report was the only place they were written;
  the task that opens the section is the one that can put them there. They are
  attributed to their tasks, and 23 and 24 fill and close the section as the pile
  says.

## Not verified here (and why)

Nothing the Heltec does changed and none of the task's code is in its image, so
the bench list is 1.5's, 20's and 21's, unchanged. What is genuinely unverified
is the seam itself: no device has implemented it.

1. **The Walter's store over its flash, and a real deep sleep** — its phase 4.
   What only a real unit can answer: whether a flash region or an NVS blob is
   the right store for a few writes a day (`docs/library.md` §11 says why not a
   retained slot: 36 bytes free); whether 20 kB of scratch at namespace scope
   is affordable beside its download buffer (its p3-05 report has DIRAM at
   68 %); and whether the `restoreKept()`-before-the-first-wake order fits step
   8 of its `WakeCycle`. The test here that is closest to it is
   `AdvertsKeepTheMomentTheyWereHeardAcrossASleepThatRestartsEveryClock`, which
   boots on a platform whose `millis()` is zero and whose wall clock is unset
   until the heartbeat — the Walter with no RTC fitted.
2. **A torn write on real flash.** The torn, short and corrupt images here are a
   vector cut or flipped; the claim that a power cut mid-write leaves an image
   the next boot refuses whole rests on the CRC and the length check, which are
   tested, and on the flash leaving the rest of the bytes where they were, which
   is the consumer's store and cannot be tested from here.
3. **The dropped counters on the exit node's page.** The heartbeat's
   `resultsDropped` and `observationsDropped` now survive a sleep on a unit with
   a store; that the server's page shows them as the unit's running figures and
   not as this boot's is the server reading the same field it always read, and
   the first unit to lose a result to a small store is what proves it.
4. **The Heltec on the bench: nothing new.** Its RAM is 304 bytes larger and
   nothing it prints changed; `docs/console.md` did not move. The walk 1.5 left
   for `v0.2.0` stands as it was.
