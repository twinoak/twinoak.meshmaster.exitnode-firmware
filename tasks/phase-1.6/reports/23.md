# p1.6-23 — The interface says what it is

A consumer of the library could not say what it is (every heartbeat said
`onboard`), could not say what it cannot do (all thirteen job types were always
advertised, and a mesh with no way to carry one had no way to answer
`unsupported`), and had to fill two `MeshReply` fields nobody read. This task is
the one interface change for all four, one `CHANGELOG.md` entry, and `agent-core`
**0.3.0**. **The Heltec's behaviour does not change**: its heartbeat is byte for
byte what it was, its mesh overrides nothing new, and its RAM is the same figure
to the byte; its flash grew by 48 bytes, named below.

The design picked for *what the mesh cannot do* is the first of the two the task
offered: **`IMesh::supports(jobType)`**, asked for each of the thirteen when the
heartbeat is built and once more before a job of one of them is run. It has a
default, `true`. Why this one, and why a default, is under *The design*.

`pio test -e native`: **376 cases in 22 suites, all passing** (370 before, six
new), in the container `RUNBOOK.md` §2 names. Both boards SUCCESS with `-Werror`.
The five-configuration host matrix, the component check and the fixture check are
green.

## The design: `supports()`, asked twice, with a default

The task offered two designs. The second — a `SendResult` meaning *this mesh does
not do that*, mapped to `unsupported`, plus an advertised-types filter on
`HeartbeatIdentity` — puts the same fact in two places (a filter the consumer
fills and a result the mesh returns) that can disagree, and answers `unsupported`
only **after** `beginJob`, a login and a send have been attempted. The first puts
the fact in the one object that knows it, the mesh, and the library asks it in
the two places the fact matters:

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| `AgentLoop::buildHeartbeat` | each of the thirteen is advertised only if `mesh_->supports(kJobTypes[i])`; the extension's types follow, unfiltered, as the task asked |
| `JobRunner::runJob` | after `canRun()` and before `beginJob()`: a type the mesh declines is `Outcome::Unsupported` with one log line, the job never begun on the mesh, nothing sent |

So a declined type is not routed here at all (EXIT-NODES §9 — routing requires
the type advertised), and a job of it that arrives anyway — claimed before a
heartbeat said otherwise, or bound to this agent — is answered without a packet.
That is what the Walter's p4-02 *local door* wanted: a `trace` its UART link has
no request for, declined once in `supports()` rather than `failed` on the air.
The pile's `README.md` says that firmware's wording (`failed` vs `unsupported`)
is settled by whichever design 23 picks: it is **`unsupported`**, and the
`SendResult::Failed`-is-`failed` rule of `docs/library.md` §3.4 stands for the
packet that could not be built.

**It has a default, `true`.** A mesh that does not override it carries
everything, which is what every mesh was until now and what the in-process one
is. The alternative, a pure virtual, would have made every implementation answer
a question with only one honest answer for most of them, broken
`test_seams`' host implementations for nothing, and made the one method the
Heltec's `AgentMesh` would have had to gain a method that returns a constant.
The interface says what it is either way; this way the Heltec's mesh does not
change at all.

## The two fields

The task said: read what `JobResultReport` and the result payloads carry; if a
result field exists that one of them is the honest source of, wire it and test it
against the fixture; otherwise remove it. Read at today's `master` of
meshmaster.dk (`a8dacb6`):

- **`JobResultReport`** (`Envelopes.cs`): `clientId`, `outcome`, `startedAt`,
  `finishedAt`, `pathUsed`, `hops`, `rttMs`, `transactions`, `data`, `log`. No
  SNR, and no provenance of `pathUsed`.
- **`snr`** — *what this board measured on the reply*. The result payloads that
  carry an SNR: `StatusResult.lastSnr` and `NeighboursResult[].snr` are the
  **repeater's** figures out of its reply bytes; `Observation.snr` is the advert's,
  from `HeardAdvert`; and `TraceResult`'s last hop — this board's own hash with
  the SNR it measured on the trace coming home — is already `traceFinalSnr`,
  which in `AgentMesh::onTraceRecv` is the same `pkt->getSNR()`. So `snr` had no
  field to go into and duplicated one that has. **Removed**; `traceFinalSnr`
  stays, with its reader.
- **`viaPath`** — *the reply came back as a returned path*. `DiscoverResult.path.source`
  is one of `PathSources` (`advert`, `login`, `discovery`, …), and the runner
  sets it from **which step** succeeded — `kPathSourceLogin` or
  `kPathSourceDiscovery` — reading `hasPath()` after the send; a flood is sent only
  when no path is held, so *a path is held after a flooded login* and *the reply
  came via path* are the same fact, and `hasPath()` is the one the discover job
  already reads. **Removed.**

`AgentMesh::takeResponse` and `takeText` lose the `mesh::Packet*` they took only
for `getSNR()`, and `takeResponse` the `bool viaPath` it took only to copy; the
two MeshCore callbacks that called them unname their now-unused `pkt`. The
Walter's link carries `via_path` and `snr_q4`; `docs/library.md` §11 now says
they, like `elapsed_ms` and `rssi`, have no field in `MeshReply` and are the
Walter's to print.

## Deviations from the spec or the task

- **The fixtures carry one transport word, so the library validates nothing.**
  The task says *validate it against the transports meshmaster.dk accepts
  (EXIT-NODES / `AgentTransports`) in a test against the contract fixtures, not a
  hand-written list*. The contract fixtures (`MANIFEST.txt` at `0595dc2`, 47
  files) carry `transport` in two — `AgentHeartbeat.json` and `AgentConfig.json`
  — and both say `onboard`. `serial`, `tcp` and `native` are in EXIT-NODES §2,
  in `Envelopes.cs`'s doc comment and in `AgentTransports.All` in
  `src/MeshMaster.Core/Transport/Agents.cs`, and in **no fixture**. So the test
  holds what the fixtures can hold — the two defaults are the fixtures' word —
  and the library holds **no list**: a word the server does not know is the
  server's to refuse (`AgentTransports.IsKnown` is its rule), and a list typed
  here would be the second copy of a vocabulary the task says not to hand-write,
  which is also what 19's report said about it. The one word a consumer needs,
  `native`, is tested as sent and read back, not validated. **For meshmaster.dk**,
  if the reviewer wants this end to hold the vocabulary: a fixture that lists
  `AgentTransports.All` (an `AgentTransports.json` written by the contracts'
  tests) would let this test be exactly what the task asked for.
- **A `trace` job the mesh declines is still priced by a wake.** `wakeJobs`
  prices every claimed job with `transactionsOf(job)` before `runClaimed`, and a
  type the mesh cannot carry costs zero transactions in fact but one in the
  estimate, so a wake with no room left would report it `failed` with *wake
  budget* rather than `unsupported`. Not changed here: a declined type is never
  advertised, so the server does not route it, and the pricing is about jobs that
  will go on the air. If a bound job of a declined type ever reaches a tight
  wake, the honest answer is a one-line check in `wakeJobs`; said here so the
  reviewer can decide whether 24 or a follow-up wants it.
- **`docs/library.md` §2's `v0.2.0` pin examples and `docs/upstream.md`'s are
  left for 24**, which rewrites §2 for a private repository; `README.md`'s *Where
  things stand at `v0.2.0`* likewise, since `v0.3.0` is tagged after 24. The
  three manifests, `CHANGELOG.md`, §3, §6, §7.1, §11, §12 and `docs/host.md` moved
  here.
- **Nothing of meshmaster.dk disagreed with the task.** EXIT-NODES §2 (the
  transport words), §5 (self-description; the fourth shape, `native`), §9 step 1
  (candidates *advertise the job type*), D-45, `Envelopes.cs` (`AgentHeartbeat`,
  `JobResultReport`), `Results.cs` (`DiscoverResult`, `TraceResult`) and
  `JobTypes.cs` (`PathSources`) were read at today's `origin/master`
  (**`a8dacb6`**). The task file cites *EXIT-NODES §4 (routing requires the type
  advertised)*; that sentence is in **§9**, step 1, and §4 is the association
  flow — a section number in the task file, not a disagreement of substance.

## Decisions taken without asking

- **`supports`, not `serves` or `canRun`.** `serves` is meshmaster.dk's word for
  an exit node's *policy* (`serves: own | all | list`, EXIT-NODES §9) and
  `canRun()` is the runner's class rule; the outcome the answer becomes is
  `unsupported`, so the question is `supports`.
- **The extension's types are not asked of the mesh**, as the task said; they
  are advertised after the thirteen exactly as before, and the runner's
  `offered` branch skips the mesh question for them as it skips `canRun()`.
- **The log line is one sentence with the type in it**, in the shape of the
  existing *no handler for job type '%s'* line, so a reader of the job's page
  sees the same kind of sentence for the two kinds of `unsupported`. The quote
  is written as the .NET encoder writes it (`'`), which is why the loop test
  holds the sentence by its two halves.
- **`FakeMesh::cannotServe(type)` adds to a set and there is no un-do**, because
  a mesh's capabilities do not change under a test, and a test that needs both
  makes two fixtures. It carries no `// answer:` citation because it is not an
  answer of the repeater's; `Citations.EveryAnswerOfTheFakeCitesAFixtureOrAFirmwareFunction`
  is green.
- **`Pending::viaPath` in `FakeMesh` became `teachesPath`**: it is the fake's own
  note that a flooded reply comes home along a path and should teach one, which
  it still does; renamed so that a grep of the tree for the removed field finds
  nothing but the note that says it is gone.
- **`MeshReply`'s comment names what left and where the readers are**, so a
  consumer whose build breaks at `reply.viaPath = …` reads why in the struct
  rather than in a changelog.
- **`CHANGELOG.md`'s `v0.3.0` section answers the four questions once, at the
  top, for the whole section**, and then lists each task's lines — 24 adds the
  manifest commit's answer in the same place. A consumer reads one paragraph and
  knows what to change; that paragraph is written as that.
- **The seam test is a text test** over `IMesh.h`, `AgentLoop.cpp`,
  `JobRunner.cpp`, `AgentMesh.h`, `AgentMesh.cpp` and `FakeMesh.h`, in the shape
  of 19's `AnUnknownTypeIsOfferedToTheExtensionBeforeItIsUnsupported`, so that
  *asked before `beginJob`* and *the Heltec's mesh overrides nothing* are held by
  name and not by inference from a behaviour test.
- **`docs/library.md` §12's suite counts moved** (45 → 46, 45 → 47) with the
  cases, since the page cites them.
